## 1. Figure: adjustment, units and reference

**Code finding — the proposed adjustment has the wrong sign if “trip worth” means the positive performance bonus.**

Let \(q=0.2\,s_\text{margin}\) and \(a_i=A[role_i]-b_\text{depth}depth_i\). Before clock limits, a positive \(a_i\) makes the horse faster by \(q a_i\). Removing that advantage means:

\[
\text{neutral time}_i=\text{time}_i+q a_i,
\]

not subtracting it. Bad trips then receive a faster adjusted figure; good trips receive a slower one.

**Code finding — even that is only a partial adjustment.**

- Closing roles change the **kick coefficient**; grit depends on the opponent.
- The winner’s time is anchored to the **winner’s `best_mile`**, not its performance score. The same additive correction therefore does not neutralize winners and losers consistently.
- Clock limits and propagated delays modify model margins. `WITHIN=.1` also makes those limits depend heavily on the winner.

Thus a median `FIG_REF` can align the aggregate median while leaving systematic errors by horse ability, field strength, role and finishing position.

**A reference role does not solve this.** Replacing \(q a_i\) with \(q(a_i-a_\text{ref})\) merely changes the constant within a track. Use a reference role for explanation if useful, not as a calibration cure.

**Change the build:**
- Define the estimand: **trip-adjusted performance**, or **estimated own clean-mile ability**. They are not automatically the same.
- Convert raw times through the actual clock mapping before comparing with displayed `best_mile` and `MILE_TIERS`; verify the half allowance is applied exactly once.
- Persist race-time bars/`best_mile`, engine version and adjustment components. Do not compare historical starts with today’s bars.
- Treat legacy clock-only figures as a separate, explicitly approximate series.
- On held-out seasons, plot figure-minus-race-time-`best_mile` by track, role, ability, winner/loser and `limited`. **Only accept one constant if those conditional biases are acceptably small.** Fit and freeze it on other seasons.

Also calibrate the **badge statistic**, not merely individual figures: selecting unusually fast performances introduces another offset.

## 2. Badge: what it actually promises

**Code/math finding:** “median of the best three” is simply **the second-fastest figure**.

That is a reasonable **recent demonstrated peak**, not a reading of current ability.

- It rejects one freak fast result, but two exceptional results can dominate.
- Following a sudden decline, two old fast figures can preserve the badge through **eight subsequent starts**, dropping out on the ninth. Infrequent racers can retain it for seasons.
- Retired second-best-ever rewards opportunity: longer careers get more chances at exceptional figures.
- Five starts need not provide three valid figures when breaks are excluded. Define eligibility by usable evidence, and specify whether the window means ten starts or ten valid figures.

**Hypothesis:** this will tell improvement and prime better than decline. Test one challenger alongside it—a recency-weighted figure estimate—rather than polishing this choice without comparison. Label this candidate **recent peak** if retained.

**Code finding — the unproven route is not “one ruler.”** It is an age-relative percentile mapped onto a changing adult badge distribution. An unchanged youngster can change badge because its peers or the proven population changed. Race five can produce a discontinuity without new evidence of comparable magnitude.

Build choice: either show an absolute predicted-time badge with an uncertainty label, or retain the peer badge and label it **crop standing**. Do not claim equivalence. For juvenile predictions, validate against later performance; age-normalized bars alone do not establish that prediction.

## 3. The decisive 100-season comparison

Your proposed checks are useful but insufficient. **Tier stability is not accuracy**, and purses reflect opportunities and class placement, which still use `est`.

Compute both badges on the **same recorded careers**, using only information available at each date:

| Measurement | What it decides |
|---|---|
| Badge/figure versus contemporary clean-mile ability **and separately** standardized rerun winning ability | Whether the badge measures speed, competitiveness, or neither reliably |
| Future clean times, wins and purses over a fixed horizon, stratified by age and opportunity/class | Whether it tells players something useful prospectively |
| Rise/peak/decline detection: delay in **starts and seasons**, missed declines, false demotions | Whether it follows a career rather than noise |
| Tier flips and reversals during genuinely stable ability periods | Whether the badge is readable |
| Fourth-to-fifth-start jumps; unproven badge versus later proven performance | Whether the provisional path is calibrated |
| Retired badge error versus career length | Whether “prime” mostly rewards more attempts |

Define decline independently—sustained deterioration in standardized ability, not worsening figures themselves. Include non-racers and retirees as censored cases rather than silently dropping them.

Report paired differences with horse/cohort-clustered uncertainty and show Patrick the **largest disagreement careers**, not just aggregate shares.

**Recommendation:** fix the figure’s sign, units and winner-anchor bias first. Then spend the second review on paired career evidence. A prettier distribution cannot rescue the wrong ruler.
