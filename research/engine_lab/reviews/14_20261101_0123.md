## (a) Ranked mechanisms and cheapest decisive experiments

### 1. Turn cost disproportionately exhausts movers—and the empty-tank floor is inconsistent

At 46.7 ft/s, the new turn term adds approximately:

- **Half-mile rail:** `1.02 × .0007 × 46.7³ / 168.5 ≈ 0.43` sustainable-power units.
- **Mile rail:** approximately **0.17**.
- Drafting saves approximately **0.153** at that speed.

Thus on the half-mile, the new turn cost is almost **three times the drafting saving**. It is not a small clock correction.

A first-over move begins at **2,904 ft**, just before the half-mile’s third turn at **2,940 ft**. It immediately asks for maximum speed, pays cubic turn cost, and later pays another turn. The leader waits until **3,880 ft** to drive. This is a credible mechanism for exhausting challengers before they arrive.

Worse, at `E == 0`, `ceiling()` returns **straight, exposed sustainable speed**, regardless of curvature or duel cost. A horse can continue producing unsustainable turn workload indefinitely while negative reserve expenditure is discarded by the clamp.

**Cheapest experiment:** two identical horses on prescribed, unobstructed paths, with breaks off. Sweep commitment at 2,904 versus 3,880 ft; compare current turn cost versus zero **without recalibrating clocks**. Record reserve, ceiling and integrated rail-progress advantage. Separately hold an empty horse on a turn and check whether demand exceeds sustainable power.

**Fix the empty-reserve inconsistency before interpreting any fatigue fit.** Do not simply erase turn cost to improve trip percentages.

### 2. Passing requires much more space than the sitting controller leaves

At approximately 50 ft/s:

- Normal equal-speed following requires about **6 ft clear**.
- Lateral admission uses `back.v + 2`, `front.v − 2`, plus another foot: approximately **32 ft clear**.
- Including the body, that is roughly **46 ft nose-to-nose**.

This is a large admission barrier beside a tightly packed line. It can make a physically capable challenger unable to enter a useful passing corridor. On the mile, the long stretch can give the full-reserve pocket horse enough time to escape; on the half-mile, the shorter stretch may preserve the leader.

**Cheapest experiment:** save 20–40 pre-drive states and use a scripted leader trajectory. Compare the subject’s normal route with a continuously clear, prescribed route, **holding exposure and path length fixed**. Log requested/granted lateral movement, blocked seconds and subject finish time.

Do not repeat race2’s mistake of removing traffic and inadvertently changing shelter too.

### 3. The driver confounds “start moving,” “leave cover” and “spend everything”

First-over receives `v=cap` from 0.55 onward. Other horses broadly receive `v=cap` from 1,400 ft out. There is no remaining-distance expenditure controller.

Also, that drive threshold is based on **each horse’s own progress**: the leader starts accelerating earlier in wall-clock time. Trailing horses wait longer before responding. Cover following then stops at the **start of the last turn**, potentially exposing a follower exactly where expenditure becomes expensive.

**Cheapest experiment:** from saved states, keep paths fixed and compare:

1. Current commands.
2. Field-event drive activation rather than individual threshold activation.
3. A small prescribed pace menu for the mover, including a controlled advance before full drive.

If a moderate advance beats “ceiling immediately,” this is controller incompetence, not inadequate horse capability.

### 4. Reserve saturation can make “15% drafting” decisive

The relevant comparison is not “15% is small.” It is saving approximately 0.15 power units for tens of seconds against a **12-unit reserve**, followed by clipping at `e_max`.

Measure **reserve differences and ceiling differences at commitment**, including how long each horse was reserve-saturated. The mile pocket can retain a full tank while exposed rivals accumulate significant expenditure.

**Cheapest experiment:** scripted pocket/leader trajectories, shelter on/off × exit open/blocked. Keep the leader’s path and pace fixed. This separates energy value from access value.

### 5. Ground loss versus Kick is plausible, but measure the recovery budget first

One extra lane costs **π × 7 ≈ 22 ft per semicircle**. A one-SD Kick advantage gives only **0.64 ft/s at full reserve**, less when tired. Over 25 seconds that buys at most about **16 ft**, before accounting for the original deficit.

That makes “outside cannot catch” plausible, but does **not** establish that Kick should be enlarged: reserve advantage and earlier positioning also matter.

Record:

\[
\text{recoverable deficit}=\int(\dot s_{\rm challenger}-\dot s_{\rm leader})\,dt
\]

First compute an optimistic fresh-ceiling bound; then actual recovery, partitioned into command, ceiling, acceleration and traffic limits. **Do not alter exact ground geometry to manufacture passing.**

## (c) Concrete code defects, ranked

### Blocking correctness

**1. Driver decisions do not all read one snapshot.**  
Commands are deferred, but `self.state` is mutated during the horse loop. Later horses inspect already-updated rivals’ `phase` for cover and crossing decisions. `by[0]` also depends on input order. `max(live, key=s)` picks the first input horse when everyone starts level.

**Fix:** snapshot horse **and driver** state, propose all state/command updates, then commit; specify meaningful tie resolution.

**2. Safety still overrides the braking limit.**  
After logging a breach, `v_new = min(v_new, vmax)` applies the excessive braking anyway. Consequently zero overlaps can be purchased by illegal deceleration.

**Fix:** prevent entry into unsafe states through jointly feasible movement decisions; retain breach traces as hard failures, not acceptable residuals.

**3. Swept lateral bands are not full swept-body collision checks.**  
Lateral admission uses current longitudinal positions and a fixed speed margin; forward following then checks final lateral positions. That does not prove separation throughout simultaneous forward/lateral movement. Braking distance is also expressed using physical speeds against rail-coordinate gaps without a complete curvature-aware derivation.

**Fix:** test continuous trajectories in consistent geometry, including turn entry/exit and partial lateral overlap.

**4. There is no actual committed lane-change state.**  
`move_y` can change each step; rounded `k_now`, `slower_ahead`, turn limits and tuck-in logic can redirect an unfinished maneuver. Rejection sets `vy=0` instantly, violating the stated lateral acceleration limit.

**Fix:** committed maneuver destination/profile, feasible stopping behavior, and explicit completion. Aim commitment is not maneuver commitment.

**5. Empty reserve does not enforce sustainable workload.**  
As above: the cap ignores turn/draft/duel conditions at exhaustion. Fix this before fitting fatigue.

### Material behavior/reporting defects

- **Gate sorting lasts through the entire first turn:** `h.s < first_turn_end` keeps non-leavers in the special start controller until **820 ft half-mile / 1,640 ft mile**. That is a major track-dependent intervention, not merely a gate rule.
- **Failure to clear is budget-based, not turn-feasibility-based.** `leave_until=.3` permits a lead attempt well beyond turn entry. This does not implement 0.8.
- **`move_close` and `last16` are unused.** There is no implemented sixteenth recheck; several promised state-machine behaviors are absent.
- **Following cover ends at last-turn entry.** That should be an opportunity/pace decision, not unconditional geometry-triggered abandonment.
- **`park_e` integrates with `R.DT`, not the actual run timestep.** DT convergence tests would change the effective parking budget. Use elapsed simulation time.
- **Traffic break pressure is largely unreachable during legal steady following:** traffic requires a clear gap below **4 ft**, while safe equal-speed headway around race pace exceeds **5 ft**. “Boxed” pressure is absent.
- **Finished horses disappear from collision/cover physics immediately.** Continue their run-out trajectories; otherwise close finishers can gain phantom space or lose cover.
- **The auditor over-accepts gate exceptions:** merely never moving outward qualifies as “working in,” including a stationary wide horse. It also checks endpoint overlaps only and ignores pairs involving a finisher.
- **`plan()` crashes for a one-horse field:** unconditional `g[1]`.
- Existing freshness/training integration is not visible in these files; verify it before treating clocks or ability tests as launch-representative.
