## Astra

**Verdict: accept cooperation as a driver capability; reject the proposed pair protocol as a safety invariant.** The key change should be **prepare a gap, then execute a reserved maneuver**, not “start crossing while B promises to make it safe.”

The reported trade-off shows that the current admission method is inadequate—not that safe passing inherently requires another horse to surrender position.

## 1. Q1 — Safety: the pair is insufficient

### The decisive flaw: safety is needed at first lateral overlap, not completion

With 7-ft lane spacing and 5.3-ft padded bodies, lateral overlap begins after approximately **1.7 ft** of sideways movement. Starting from rest at `AY_MAX=4`, that is roughly **0.92 seconds**. Completing the lane change takes much longer.

Consequently, “B can open the gap before M finishes crossing” can approve a collision before completion.

Also reject:

- **Exempting B from swept checks.** A cooperative horse remains a physical body.
- **Treating `A_YIELD < BRAKE` as a proof.** Small planned deceleration does not guarantee adequate separation, particularly if M brakes, another horse merges, or B already has insufficient headway.
- **Expiring an underway reservation and settling in the nearest lane.** This can reverse a committed move, release occupied space and create a second unsafe merge.
- **“Forward pass as now” as the safety foundation.** The current pass can detect infeasibility without preventing it.

### Simplest structure I would build

Separate **tactical consent** from **physical admissibility**, using:

`REQUESTED → PREPARING → COMMITTED → COMPLETE`

1. **Snapshot:** all physical states, driver states and existing reservations.
2. **Request:** M names an adjacent destination and a candidate longitudinal slot. F/B are slot boundaries, not the only affected horses.
3. **Prepare:** M stays in its current corridor. M adjusts speed; B may voluntarily ease. Cancellation is allowed here.
4. **Commit:** admit only when the joint trajectory is safe from **first contact through completion**, with a feasible emergency continuation.
5. **Execute:** preserve the destination and reserve both occupied corridors until M clears them. Replan pace, not maneuver ownership.
6. **Complete:** release the old corridor only after physical clearance.

Use a conflict graph to select compatible requests. Initially, allow **at most one active maneuver role per horse**; still check its effects on all followers. Multiple independent merges can proceed simultaneously.

The actual invariant is:

> Every accepted update preserves nonintersection and a feasible bounded-braking/lateral-stopping continuation for all affected bodies.

That continuation must account for M or F breaking stride, source-lane followers, destination-lane followers and turn geometry. A reservation prevents others from entering its protected space; it cannot assume other horses will never brake.

**Important:** an emergency may delay completion, but must not dissolve the commitment. Guarantee eventual completion only under stated feasible conditions—not against arbitrary permanent blockage.

## 2. Q3 — Clear-by: necessary context, not the safety formula

Your stated distance is approximately:

`14 + 1 + 50 × .1 = 20 ft`

That is **2.5 reporting lengths**, not a length and a half.

More importantly, it omits relative-speed braking requirements. A faster B needs more room; a slower B may need less than a fixed conservative rule suggests.

### Recommended rule

Whenever M will enter B’s occupied band:

- Establish safe longitudinal ordering by **first padded-body overlap**.
- Maintain the corresponding braking-safe gap throughout overlap.
- Check front clearance to F as well.
- Use consistent track/world geometry, not physical speeds inserted into rail-coordinate formulas without accounting for curvature.

**Outward moves do not skip rear safety.** Pulling out in front of a faster outside horse is just as dangerous. But an outward move into an empty corridor needs no fictional B and no additional clear-by requirement.

For the stretch, add a separate **non-cutting admissibility rule**: M cannot force a rival to brake to create the clearance needed for its crossing. Being mechanically collision-free is weaker than keeping a straight, fair racing line. Fanning into genuinely available space remains allowed.

## 3. Q2 — Yield policy: reject “no more than one position”

That criterion is neither a safety rule nor a good proxy for serving B:

- One position can cost the race.
- Giving up a position can improve B’s chance by obtaining cover.
- Repeated individually permitted concessions can surrender the whole field.
- “Sitting” or “leading at steady pace” does not imply consent.

Use a small rule-based comparison of B’s own options:

- Hold its corridor and pace.
- Accept useful cover.
- Avoid a costly contest.
- Concede a slot that M will safely obtain anyway.

Bound **time lost, speed reduction and repeated concessions**, with a reason logged. A short fixed-action rollout can diagnose whether the rules are making obvious sacrifices; it need not become a learned driver.

### Preventing the slinky

- Give each horse a cumulative yield budget and recovery interval, not just a per-request limit.
- B cannot promise incompatible yields to several movers.
- Include B’s following chain in the feasibility calculation.
- Use bounded, anticipatory pace changes rather than repeatedly chasing a moving gap target.
- Keep any accepted preparation plan stable unless a relevant event changes feasibility.

Do **not** recursively negotiate with every follower. Their safety controller must handle B’s permitted braking; admission must verify that they are initially in states where it can.

## 4. Q4 — Deadlocks and gate sorting

Distinguish **algorithmic deadlock** from legitimate denial of racing room.

### Opposing lane changes

Two level horses cannot simply exchange lanes. Select one request for preparation; the other holds its corridor. One must establish longitudinal separation before either crosses.

Priority should be:

1. Existing physical commitments.
2. Feasible requests.
3. Stable arbitration among conflicting feasible requests.

“Earliest request” needs expiry/revalidation **before commitment**, or a hopeless old request monopolizes a slot. “Nearer the rail” is an explicit geometric priority that will influence inside advantage; it is not neutral merely because it avoids reading `post`. Log arbitration outcomes and test their effect.

### Gate

Yielding cannot be the sole sorting mechanism. An outside horse must be able to **take back and enter behind** when an inside driver refuses.

Reserve actual slots; do not have several outside horses pursue the same apparent opening. Make the clear-versus-take-back decision early enough to remain feasible before the turn.

The merge layer can enforce safety and commitment, but **cannot alone guarantee 0.6**. That needs a gate-sorting planner, including Patrick’s allowed working-in exception and a feasible schedule for the declared starting geometry.

### Pocket

A pocket horse denied room is sometimes correctly stuck. Do not resolve that by compelling the outside horse to yield. Retry only when a genuinely new opportunity opens, and attach cooldowns to **the opportunity**, not the entire destination lane.

**Passing positive control:** M should be able to pull into an empty outside corridor without any cooperative B. If that still fails, the problem is admission geometry or execution—not negotiation.

## 5. Q5 — Acceptance gates before tuning

### Hard mechanics gates

For each case, require **zero** overlaps, braking-limit breaches, lateral-limit breaches and illegal commitment reversals—not “near zero.”

1. **First-contact boundary:** vary rear gap and relative speed just above/below feasibility; ensure approval is based on first overlap.
2. **Emergency injection:** M, F or B breaks at every stage—preparation, first overlap, midpoint and completion.
3. **Following chain:** B has several tightly following horses; verify the entire chain.
4. **Conflict cases:** two movers into one slot, opposite-direction swaps, shared F/B, three adjacent simultaneous requests.
5. **Geometry transitions:** merge at turn entry/exit, stretch fan, rail and width boundaries.
6. **Reservation interruption:** blocked preparation cancels cleanly; interrupted committed motion retains safe ownership and destination.
7. **Finish/run-out:** merging and following remain valid across the wire.

### Required progress tests

Safety alone can pass by freezing everybody. Require:

- Empty-corridor pass succeeds.
- Existing adequate gap admits without unnecessary yielding.
- Feasible preparation opens a gap and completes.
- Refusal produces a stable hold or alternative-slot attempt.
- Eight-abreast gate sorting works across representative gate traits and declared exceptions.
- Pocket-open exits; pocket-blocked remains blocked without chatter.

### Numerical and audit gates

- Input-order permutations and tied-state cases.
- DT 1/8, 1/16 and 1/32 convergence.
- Continuous swept-body checks, including lateral stopping trajectories.
- Independent auditor using unrounded simulation trajectories.
- Logs of requested slot, refusal reason, first overlap, gap minima, reservation ownership, yield cost and completion.

## Current-code defects to fix alongside this work

1. **`held_speed()` cache collision:**  
   `h.duel and round(h.t["grit"], 3)` produces `False` when not dueling and `0.0` for a dueling zero-Grit horse. Those compare equal as dictionary keys. Use separate `(bool(h.duel), grit)` fields. Otherwise cache results can depend on evaluation order.

2. **Braking remains inconsistent in rail coordinates.**  
   Passing `v*kf` into a formula using physical `BRAKE` does not generally represent rail-coordinate stopping distance, especially while curvature or lateral position changes.

3. **The longitudinal pass mixes time levels.**  
   `ahead` is identified from positions that are being updated during the pass. Freeze ordering/interaction identities from the snapshot or solve the candidate trajectories consistently.

4. **Commitments are still redirected:**  
   width-clamping `h.lc` and abandoning to the nearest lane can change a started maneuver. Replace both with the reservation lifecycle.

5. **Endpoint arrival can reset lateral velocity abruptly.**  
   Setting `vy=dist/dt` and then zero needs a bounded-acceleration arrival construction, not an endpoint correction.

**Build recommendation:** implement **prepare-then-reserve with full-body checks**, first without negotiation. Demonstrate passing into existing gaps. Then add voluntary B yielding as one way to create additional gaps. That separates safety from cooperation and avoids making “let rivals in” the hidden control for post or trip percentages.
