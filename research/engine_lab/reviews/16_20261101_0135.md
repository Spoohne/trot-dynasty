## 1. Stop the broad fit: H19 is not established

**The current result is “gate buys little under this controller,” not “position buys little.”** Code inspection gives several reasons:

- Leavers request `v_sus + leave_push`. Raising `burst_gate` does nothing whenever that request, rather than the ceiling, binds.
- Non-leavers’ `gate_pace` is frequently overridden by `inside → min(inside.v) − gate_drop` or by yielding. Increasing the coefficient need not change their actual trajectory.
- Everyone starts at 44 ft/s. Gate-dependent acceleration may operate for only a short interval before reaching the requested speed.
- The first-turn run-ins are nearly identical: **300 ft half / 320 ft mile**. The required track difference must mostly arise from the *subsequent value of position*, not a substantially different launch opportunity.
- “Gate aims frozen” still allows event changes, slot selection, following, budgets and pace responses. It is **not frozen execution**.

Two other attribution problems:

- Cruise raises sustainable power **and** `v_sus`, which sets several requested paces and budgets. Its measured value includes controller effects.
- `k_kick` changes `ceiling()` throughout the race, not just in the drive. Check whether it is buying opening position before calling its effect “kick.”

**First deliverable:** for baseline/+gate pairs, report actual opening acceleration, ceiling/request/safety binding time, successful clears, first-turn and quarter-mile position, extra ground and reserve. A coefficient sweep without this first-stage check is largely uninterpretable.

## 2. Fitting procedure: paired, staged, then jointly constrained

### Sampling and uncertainty

Your fitter already uses common fields and paired upgrades. With breaks off, these evaluations are deterministic: **the ±0.12 is uncertainty across sampled fields, not rerun noise.** Repeating identical races adds nothing.

Use:

- **Pilot/screen:** 64–128 independent fields per track, all eight subjects upgraded separately.
- Balance subject posts and opening aims; use post rotations on a diagnostic subset. Cluster rotations and subjects under their originating field.
- **Untouched validation:** start with 256–512 independent fields per track; expand according to measured precision, not a predetermined magic number.
- Estimate SE from **field-level mean gains**. Target roughly **0.03–0.04-place confidence half-widths**, preferably narrower near a contract boundary.

Sample size scales with inverse squared precision. If ±0.12 and ±0.04 denote the same uncertainty measure, you need approximately **nine times the independent information**. If 0.12 is an SE but 0.04 is a 95% half-width, approximately **35 times**. Using eight rather than four subjects does not double independent information.

For manners, use many paired stochastic continuations within fields, but retain field clustering. Prefer randomness keyed by horse/time/event: currently skipping RNG calls while broken causes paired streams to diverge after different break outcomes.

### Order and knobs

| Stage | Fit/check | Main knobs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sustainable pace, turn workload, reserve trajectory, achievable drive speeds | baseline power/speed scale, `c_turn`, `v_top`, `e_max`, `recover`, `fade` |
| 2 | Gate actually acquires useful positions at plausible cost | `a_gate`, `burst_gate`; launch duration justified by launch behavior; controller access/slot rules separately |
| 3 | Trait sensitivities on that functioning baseline | `p_cruise`, `k_kick`, `k_grit`, gate coefficients |
| 4 | Joint track-shaped contract, clocks, adaptive sign and mechanism gates | small constrained refinement of identifiable knobs |

Important distinctions:

- **`duel` sets the cost of dueling; `k_grit` sets resistance to it.** Do not inflate duel cost merely to manufacture grit value.
- Likewise, reserve capacity/fade determine race physiology; they should not be free compensation knobs for any failed stat.
- Do not use `c_turn` merely to create the required 2.5-second clock gap. That already produced reserve starvation.
- Fit baseline clocks alongside reserve/workload diagnostics. `pace_k` is **not a neutral clock adjustment**: it changes the whole spending economy.

Then estimate a paired finite-difference **sensitivity matrix**: each metric against each knob. Use a small response-surface/trust-region search, including selected interactions, rather than another 12-dimensional scatter. A flat gate column calls for a mechanism investigation, not a larger search radius.

### Should frozen-aim places be fitted at all?

**Yes—as Patrick’s declared contract and an interaction check. No—as the sole single-horse calibration target.**

Use three distinct assays:

1. Scripted solo/controlled workload: what capability changed?
2. Frozen opening aims: what does that capability buy under the declared policy?
3. Adaptive driving: can the driver exploit it without making the mean worse?

Gate especially is an *option to acquire position*. A policy that never exercises the option can make a useful capability appear worthless. Keep the frozen-aim requirement unless Patrick changes it, but don’t distort physiology to compensate for an incompetent policy.

## 3. Test H19 by separating acquisition, positional value and price

Run these in order:

### A. Does gate change position?

Use the opening measurements above. Stratify by post and aim.

- No first-turn/quarter-mile improvement → **gate acquisition/controller problem**.
- Improvement that disappears before the drive → **position-retention or tactical problem**.
- Improvement retained but no finish benefit → investigate positional value and its cost.

### B. Is better position valuable at equal resources?

Construct legal saved states at first-turn exit and the quarter:

- Identical-trait horses occupying two existing legal slots.
- Swap which horse occupies the forward/rearward slot.
- Equalize the compared horses’ reserve and speed; initialize driver state consistently, without mid-merge transplants.
- Continue with the same declared policy and paired randomness.

Test **lead, pocket, forward covered line, rear covered line and first-over separately**. “Position” is not one scalar: being one place farther forward but exposed is a different treatment.

Report finish-time/place gains, remaining ground, blocked time and reserve spent. Repeat at several field pace/reserve conditions.

### C. Does acquiring it cost more than it returns?

In real baseline/+gate races, compare normal continuations against a **subject-reserve-equalized diagnostic** at the checkpoint. This isolates the contribution of reserve expenditure within those branches; it is not itself a pure position intervention.

Interpretation:

- Position valuable at equal reserve, but actual gate gain worthless → acquisition is too costly or poorly chosen.
- Position not valuable even at equal reserve → investigate passing/recovery geometry and continuation behavior.
- Some slots valuable, others harmful → improve slot selection; don’t increase “front-end value” globally.

### Mechanisms worth testing—without bonuses

The legitimate advantages already have physical sources: less distance left, fewer bodies to pass, shorter paths around turns, access to cover and limited time to find an exit.

The most promising driver change is **choosing an attainable forward slot rather than “lead or drop back”**, using gate capability to clear or occupy it, then settling instead of surrendering the gain. Pair that with workload-aware pace management so a leader need not burn its reserve following a straight-line pace prescription through tight turns.

Do **not** make position valuable by artificially delaying rear horses. Better early position need not always pay; it should pay in identifiable circumstances.

## 4. Contract: measurable after clarification; joint feasibility still unproved

**There is no arithmetic contradiction in those place targets.** The upgrades occur in separate counterfactual races, so their gains need not sum to a fixed budget.

But “eight starters, frozen aims” does not fully define the estimand. Freeze:

- Trait distribution, correlations and class spread.
- Post assignment and weighting.
- Baseline aim-generation policy and precisely what remains adaptive.
- Upgrade size, subjects sampled, tracks and geometry.
- Which population supplies the clock medians.

An expected rank gain depends strongly on opponent strength and finish-margin density. It is not an intrinsic unit of a trait.

**No current evidence establishes that one physically plausible parameter set can meet all eight trait cells plus both clocks.** The main risks are:

- Half/mile gate reversal versus kick reversal under shared coefficients.
- Cruise/clock/reserve coupling.
- Grit value limited by consequential duel exposure.
- Manners value limited by rare preventable breaks.

For manners, approximately:

\[
\text{place benefit} \approx
\text{reduction in break probability}\times
\text{mean places lost per break}.
\]

At a 4% break rate, reducing breaks by one percentage point and saving four places per prevented break buys only **0.04 places**. The 0.12 target therefore deserves an explicit feasibility check before fitting hazard coefficients.

Acceptance should be **confidence intervals inside each tolerance band**, with appropriate simultaneous coverage for the final claims—not merely a low summed loss. Also, pooled median clocks do not replace the spec’s tier-clock requirement.

### What I would reject

1. **H19 as a finding**, before demonstrating that the gate intervention actually improves position.
2. More broad random search on the same 12 fields, or selecting candidates from unversioned historical logs.
3. Buying trait balance through excessive duels, reserve starvation, guaranteed pocket exits, artificial traffic or track-specific stat bonuses.
4. Treating frozen opening aims as frozen behavior, or positive frozen effects as proof of adaptive competence.
5. Declaring success from aggregate loss while one contract cell fails.
6. Treating the attached record as a clean safety certificate: item 138 still reports breaches. Keep safety/progress rejection active for every candidate; higher acceleration and speed can invalidate earlier checks.

**Recommended next run:** the gate first-stage diagnostic plus the equal-resource slot-swap assay. Those distinguish “unused gate capability,” “expensive acquisition,” and “low positional value” before you spend another large batch fitting the wrong cause.
