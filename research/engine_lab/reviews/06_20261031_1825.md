## 1. H9: plausible, but two code confounds prevent accepting it yet

**Code findings:**

- **`clear_path` also removes shelter.** Setting `blockers=[]` empties `ahead_same`, which disables the aerodynamic saving and following behavior. The closer’s extra energy consumption therefore cannot be attributed solely to spending its reserve on an unobstructed run. Separate **physical obstruction**, **aerodynamic cover**, and **driver following**.
- **The surplus-kick test does not actually request the surplus speed.** `top` includes `surplus`, but the drive target remains the original `SPRINT + trait terms`; `min(target, h.top)` clips the request to that original ceiling. Thus failure of `surplus_k` is not evidence against a stronger fresh-horse kick.

Also, **1.05 seconds behind the winner is not absolute subject time**: the intervention can change the winner’s time too. Report paired subject times and opponent times separately.

Your other concerns are valid **code properties**:
- At a constant seven-length gap, the closer approximately matches the leader’s speed—not a slower, conserving pace.
- Below cruise, speed reduction saves no energy within the current cost formula, holding shelter/lane/stamina fixed.

**H9 should remain:** “The late trip may surrender more distance than its energy advantage can recover.” Neither “traffic is unimportant” nor “leaders barely fatigue” is isolated yet.

## 2. Minimal mechanism tests

Before adding physiology, calculate the recovery budget: seven lengths is **56 feet**. Recovering that over 25 seconds requires roughly **2.2 ft/s average speed advantage**, before extra ground. Log actual gap at commitment, remaining time, speed advantage and its integral. That identifies exactly where recovery fails.

Use identical horses and prescribed opening trajectories, then release them into an unobstructed finish. All proposed changes below are **hypotheses**, not calibrated realism.

| Candidate | Decisive paired test |
|---|---|
| **(a) Exposure versus following** | Same speed, distance and acceleration history; toggle only aerodynamic shelter. Compare accumulated expenditure, then release from identical positions to measure the usable finish advantage. Apply exposure to **every unsheltered horse**, including an isolated closer—not to the “leader” label. |
| **(b) Stronger freshness response** | From identical three-quarter states, sweep energy and compare current versus steeper fatigue curves. Ensure the requested speed can reach the tested ceiling. Then repeat with the actual leader/closer energy states and deficit. Prefer fresh horses retaining an ability-defined maximum longer over an unbounded surplus-speed bonus. |
| **(c) Smaller sitting gap** | Sweep desired gap **3/5/7 lengths**, holding move timing and traits fixed. Separately run a snapshot test varying only initial separation, with energy/speed equal. This distinguishes reduced catch-up distance from changed expenditure or cover exposure. |
| **(d) Steeper high-speed cost** | Script equal-distance openings with steady versus fast-then-slow pacing; compare existing and steeper cost curves, then release. The added cost must apply to **all horses producing that speed**, not just leaders. Confirm that a chasing closer also pays. |

**Minimal physically motivated change to test first, after the fixes:** split expenditure into a speed-dependent locomotion term and an exposure-dependent aerodynamic term. Shelter should reduce the aerodynamic component, not all metabolic cost equally. Aerodynamic power increasing approximately with airspeed cubed is a defensible starting shape, but its coefficient needs calibration.

Do not add an unexplained “pace-setting tax.” If pressing causes extra accelerations, measure and charge those accelerations. And do not require seven-length closers to win equally under every pace: viability should emerge in suitable race shapes.
