## Verdict

**Trips first, then performance—but this draft is not yet an identifiable specification.** “About 25 parameters” understates the model unless several trip rules and weight constraints are fixed. More importantly, the targets do not identify every latent mechanism.

No honest review can promise a minimal model that hits every target before testing feasibility. Here is the smallest structure I would test.

## 1. Trips first; no independent performance-first draw

Use this causal order:

**traits + form → early positions → pace/pressure and trips → remaining performance → finish.**

Performance-first would require inventing trips retrospectively, making gate, cruise and grit harder to interpret. Outcome-first means deciding the result before rendering—not deciding it before its causes.

The draft needs actual intermediate states: **positions at the half and roles at three-quarters**, not one permanent `trip_i`. Otherwise it cannot measure the rail-at-half targets or the contracted winning-trip classification.

## 2. Minimal candidate

Start with:

\[
e_i = gate_i+f_i-c_t(post_i-1)+\sigma_s z_i
\]

\[
P_i=w_{c,t}x_{c,i}+w_{k,t}x_{k,i}
+A_{t,r_i}+gD_i(x_{g,i}-x_{g,j})+\sigma_\epsilon u_i
\]

Here:

- \(x\) is the effective trait after applying the agreed form rule.
- \(r\) is one of the **five contracted three-quarter roles**.
- \(D\) identifies an actual duel and its rival.
- Breaks apply the calibrated safety process and a fixed severe penalty.
- **No direct gate coefficient.** Gate earns places through position.
- **No full `W[track,trip,trait]` table.**

Fix one role offset per track to zero and fix one positive weight normalization, such as \(w_{c,\mathrm{half}}+w_{k,\mathrm{half}}=1\), to remove location and scale ambiguity.

With two post slopes, start/form/residual SDs, four cruise/kick weights minus that normalization, eight role contrasts, one grit coefficient and two margin scales, that is **19 fitted Part 1 parameters**. Time calibration is separate.

Initially **fix** duel, parking, movement, following and boxing rules as explicit design choices. The supplied aggregates cannot reliably identify all their probabilities *and* the corresponding trip rewards. If those rules must be estimated, add observations of trip occupancy, transitions and pressure—not just winning trips.

Only if needed, add two shared interactions:

- cruise × workload;
- kick × closing opportunity.

That gives a low-rank `W`, not a table. Start without them because the numerical targets are mostly marginal; they do not establish a need for trip-specific trait weights.

**Caveat:** this is a parsimonious candidate, not proof of feasibility or identifiability. Verify both numerically.

### Fix these ambiguities first

- Form currently enters the start, “every trait,” and performance separately. Specify one convention. Under the contract’s effective-trait convention, **do not add it again** to performance.
- Parked, boxed and broke are states/modifiers, not additional winning-trip categories. Classify the winner using the contracted geometry.
- Pressure-dependent breaking must preserve the manners calibration on its specified population.
- Add the tier shift explicitly; the displayed performance equation currently omits it.

## 3. Conflicts and constraints

**Repeat versus best:** for each fixed field,

\[
R=\sum_i p_i^2\leq \max_i p_i.
\]

Thus 45% repeat forbids a 30% true-best win rate on the same population. **50–55% is plausible, not independently mandatory.** CONTRACTS explicitly says report it rather than fit it.

**Repeat versus favorite:** a 35–40% favorite rate conflicts with 45% repeat **if favorite means true best on the same population**. The amended game-odds definition avoids that conflict. Do not quietly use the lab proxy as its acceptance test.

**Gate target:** the supplied contract still says **0.05**, whereas this spec asks for **0.45/0.20**. That requires a logged amendment before tuning. Also freeze the intervention protocol for “places gained”: selected horse, population, paired randomness and whether all downstream decisions rerun.

**Photo versus median margin:** one margin multiplier cannot independently set both. It rescales a fixed gap distribution. Check whether any scale satisfies both targets; if not, change the performance-gap distribution—not the measurement definition.

**No other numerical conflict is established from the supplied targets.** In particular, rail-at-half and post-win targets concern different events. Their compatibility needs simulation.

The rail-at-half percentages also need tolerances and a precise “position” definition. Winning trips and quarter patterns ultimately require actual paths, not intended labels.

## 4. Fit it

1. **Freeze definitions and populations.** Preserve the distinct one-class, mixed-field and integrated-game checks.
2. **Run a feasibility sweep first:** repeat/best pairs, role availability, rail position versus gate benefit, and margin-scale feasibility.
3. Fit with **common random numbers**, fixed training fields and a variance-aware simulated-moments objective. Use zero loss inside accepted tolerance bands; do not chase every target’s center.
4. Fit the post effect with **one monotone slope per track**, not eight or ten post coefficients. Respect starts-per-post denominators and the live field mix.
5. Initialize in blocks—positions/trips, performance/repeat, margins—then optimize jointly. Form does **not** alone determine repeat: every refreshed random component contributes.
6. Compute the moment-to-parameter sensitivity matrix. Weak or collinear directions mean **fix or remove parameters**. More Monte Carlo does not cure non-identifiability.
7. Validate on untouched fields/seeds, with field-clustered intervals and multiple optimizer starts.

Use the contractual **K=24** ranking estimator in official reports, but also report a high-K diagnostic. Twenty-four reruns produce noisy “true best” and bottom-third labels; use independent evaluation races to avoid selection bias.

## 5. What choreography needs

Not merely finish order and margins. Supply:

- positions, lanes and gaps at the calls;
- quarter pace and segment-time feasibility bounds;
- cover, parking, boxing and passing windows;
- duel partners and timing;
- workload/remaining-capacity constraints;
- break timing;
- final order, margins and clock.

These can be coarse budgets, not another physics engine. But they must rule out impossible recoveries and passes.

**Validate contracted trips and quarter patterns on the realized race.** Report realization failures by target type. Otherwise the outcome model can “pass” while the visible race violates the specification.
