## Astra

## 1. Accept the narrower H11—not “closers fixed”

**Measured:** repaired traffic interventions substantially improve closer performance, and the existing ability model permits recovery in favorable clear-path continuations. That rejects the claim that a higher ability ceiling is *necessary* in those cases.

**Item 85 supports:** longer/wider finishing geometry reduces boxing; reducing the sitting gap improves closer wins further. Geometry alone mainly benefited stalkers: closers went **1% → 2%**, then **2% → 9%** with the gap change. Physiology still interacts with timing, as item 80 shows.

**Small isolation test:** old/new turn layout × three/five-wide allowance, at **fixed gap and move points**, followed by the gap comparison on the chosen layout. Report paired subject times, extra ground, boxed time and energy—not just style wins.

Important **code findings**:
- Drive thresholds do not automatically move with the turn layout; hard-coded **0.8, 0.85 and 0.97** rules remain. Their relationship to the bends changes.
- Wide-ground cost uses the **first turn’s length for every turn**. Use each segment’s actual curvature. For a complete semicircle, an offset \(y\) adds \(\pi y\) of path regardless of radius; audit this invariant.
- `route` can angle a horse outward, then the ordinary tuck-in rule can bring it back next step. Its null result does not establish that anticipatory route-finding is useless.

## 2. Fit: a small screen after one pocket check; acceptance needs geometry finished

**H:** stalkers benefit from an excellent package—short deficit, shelter and easy release. That may be legitimate, exaggerated or partly an implementation artifact.

Run one **pocket diagnostic** before tuning it away: identical scripted leader/blocker trajectories, **shelter discount on/off × exit open/blocked**, with the subject’s initial state fixed. Measure energy saved, release delay and absolute finish time. Rotate posts in subsequent full-field validation.

There is **no dedicated inside passing lane in the attached code**; distinguish that venue feature from permission to fan outward.

### First-stage free parameters
- Desired gaps and move points.
- `lead_soft`, `lead_dig`.
- `break_k`, calibrated against break metrics rather than style balance.

### Initially fixed
- Venue geometry, width, turn-distance calculation and collision rules.
- Acceleration, fatigue curve, energy costs and trait-speed coefficients.
- `budget=0`, `surplus_k=0`; freeze the chosen split setting.

Only if driver-policy fitting cannot satisfy the gates should a second, narrow search open **shelter and kick coefficients**, with independent shelter/class tests. I would **not freely tune `cost_wide`**: establish what it represents beyond extra distance and aerodynamic exposure first, or it becomes an arbitrary outside-trip penalty.

Treat style balance as a **soft target across several field compositions and venues**, not mandatory parity in every field. Avoid obtaining parity merely by making all styles behave identically.

**Acceptance blocker:** lane changes are still instantaneous in this attachment. Implement finite lateral motion and swept-footprint collision checks before calling any fit final. Re-run geometry, DT robustness and mechanism gates on that implementation; otherwise the fitter may exploit free lateral movement again.

## 3. Homestretch: use venue data, not a universal fraction

Yes—make it **per venue**: circumference, turn radii/transitions, straight lengths, finish-line offset, usable width and any passing-lane opening/rules. Derive turn positions from that layout, including both laps on a half-mile oval.

For temporary synthetic venues, I would test:

- **Mile:** approximately **1,000 ft** turn exit to finish.
- **Half-mile:** approximately **500 ft** turn exit to finish.

These are **provisional modeling defaults, not verified measurements of particular venues**. Sensitivity-test roughly 660–1,200 ft and 400–660 ft respectively, then replace them with official specifications.

A **660-ft stretch is plausible as a scenario**, but should not become the universal mile-track definition. Also distinguish the entire straight from **turn exit to the wire**: the finish need not lie at its end.

**Bottom line:** stop searching for a closer-specific physiological rescue. Isolate the geometry effects, audit the pocket advantage, finish continuous movement, then calibrate tactics on fixed, credible venues.
