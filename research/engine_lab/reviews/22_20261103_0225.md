## Astra

## 1. Is `pred_mile` a fair truth?

**Fair for internal consistency; not sufficient to establish that v2 is the better badge.**

**Measured:** v2 reconstructs its own underlying score substantially better than live does. That is useful evidence. But figures and “truth” share the same weighted traits and calibration, so the comparison structurally favors v2.

`pred_mile` is also **not the engine’s full competitive ability**: gate, grit, manners and trip acquisition affect winning beyond that score. Call it **current predicted clean-mile score**, not “truth.”

**Build change:** keep this audit, but add per-start snapshots now—both badges, their scores, bars, age, class and race conditions. Judge both against a fixed horizon of **future performance**, with opportunities/class controlled. Standardized simulated fields and posts offer an additional winning-ability benchmark without purse-placement confounding.

If the promise is specifically speed, also validate against held-out `best_mile` and subsequent clean clocks, acknowledging the latter’s field dependence.

Do not claim better decline tracking: **60% versus 59% same-season detection, and 5% versus 4% long lag, show no demonstrated advantage.**

## 2. Ship median-of-six?

**Yes—for Patrick’s comparison. Keep the shipped 0.3-second hysteresis. Do not introduce another estimator without paired evidence.**

**Measured:** recent median beats peak on your internal yardstick. The 0.3-second hysteresis reduces season-pair flips from 41.1% to 33.5%, costing only 0.7 percentage points of tier agreement. That supports this candidate, although unchanged truth *tier* does not mean unchanged ability.

**Hypothesis:** six starts provide a reasonable noise/responsiveness compromise. Under independent Gaussian noise, the median’s approximate SD is **0.74 seconds on mile starts and 0.93 on half-mile starts**—material near a boundary, but much smaller than individual-start spread. Correlated freshness, changing ability and mixed tracks weaken that calculation.

Two small changes before comparison:

- **Make evidence status explicit:** fewer than three valid figures remains provisional, regardless of total starts. Show valid-figure count and recency; six old starts are not necessarily “today.”
- **Record the start-five transition.** Season-end data cannot validate the peer-share handoff, and its discontinuity remains untested.

Finally, the residual spread is measured; its attribution to form/freshness/tonic/driver is **not established by that summary alone**. Remaining grit and residual engine noise can contribute too.

**Bottom line:** ship the current statistic as a candidate, with honest evidence labels and per-start logging. The missing work is independent validation—not a cleverer smoother.
