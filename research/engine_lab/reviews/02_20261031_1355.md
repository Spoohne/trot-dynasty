## 3. Decisive race-mechanism tests

First instrument the causal chain:

**instruction → actual trip → energy expenditure → binding speed limit → finish.**

Use deterministic paired cases, then stochastic validation. Record continuous quantities, not just wins.

### (a) Energy differences do not affect the kick

**Code finding:** energy has no effect on `top` above **45**, and no further effect below **0**. Even between those thresholds, it matters only when the energy-limited top speed constrains motion.

**Test:** at three-quarters, clone an identical state into an unobstructed stretch; set subject energy to **0, 15, 30, 45, 75**. Record final-quarter time and time spent energy-limited. Then transplant the actual energies from early/late and sheltered/exposed trips into that identical stretch state.

- A clear artificial dose response but no actual-trip response supports insufficient energy separation.
- Actual energies all above 45 support saturation.
- Strong energy differences but traffic-limited speeds identify a different bottleneck.

### (b) Shelter rarely activates

**Important code finding:** `follow=False` **does not disable shelter**. Both horses receive the same energy discount whenever `ahead_same` qualifies. The existing comparison tests a pacing instruction, not shelter’s causal benefit.

**Test:** script identical speed/lane trajectories with a pacemaker at **1.1, 1.3, 1.5 and 2 lengths**. Toggle only the aerodynamic discount; measure sheltered seconds and cumulative energy cost. Then release the subject into the identical stretch test above.

Also log actual gap distributions in ordinary races. This distinguishes **rare exposure**, **a working saving with no performance consequence**, and **an ineffective following instruction**. Recompute neighborhood membership after committed lane changes first.

### (c) The second leaver gives the subject a pocket

**Test:** log leader identity, subject position, sheltered seconds, alongside-pressure seconds and quarter splits. Then compare scripted opening phases:

1. Subject leads unchallenged.
2. Rival stays alongside outside, sustaining pressure without passing.
3. Rival clears; subject follows in its pocket.

Release all drivers afterward.

**H:** if case 2 increases expenditure and hurts the subject while case 3 helps, the original “duel” is actually a favorable lead-transfer scenario. If case 2 never raises pace, investigate driver logic; if it raises pace but not fatigue, investigate energy.

**Priority:** fix DT-dependent braking and establish the forced-box positive control, then run these three mechanism tests—still no fitting.
