## 1. Half-mile geometry: reject “160 ft proves the layout is wrong”

Your layout is geometrically consistent: each lap has two **660-ft straights and two 660-ft semicircles**. A 502-ft turn-exit-to-wire stretch leaves approximately **158 ft from the wire/start to the next bend**. Short run-ins can be legitimate; circumference alone does not determine them.

More importantly, **fixed-aim rail wins are 26%, versus 52% with the driver**. That implicates driver amplification as well as geometry—not geometry alone.

**Proposed synthetic default, not a measured venue:**
- Circumference: 2,640 ft.
- Two straights: **800 ft each**.
- Two semicircular arcs: **520 ft each**, radius approximately 166 ft.
- Final turn exit to wire: **500 ft**.
- Start/wire to first bend: **300 ft**.
- Repeat the same geometry on lap two.

Sensitivity-test 200/300/400-ft run-ins using consistent layouts. Longer straights require tighter turns at fixed circumference; account for that rather than simply shifting turn boundaries. Prefer verified venue dimensions ultimately.

Keep Patrick’s 18.6% rail target as the **design benchmark**, not evidence that a particular geometry is physically correct. Refit the value model after changing geometry.

## 2. Switching: there are implementation defects, but the 52% measure is not yet valid

**Code findings:**
- `len(h.trip)-1` counts narrative events, including “followed cover,” not just aim changes.
- `aim_state` and crowding read mutable horses after earlier horses have already updated. Decisions do not share a consistent snapshot.
- Spend-limit switches bypass hysteresis. `parked_e` never resets, and `spent0` measures spending since the gate, not a renewed attempt. A driver can re-enter an already-exhausted tactic and immediately abandon it.
- Feasibility can remove the current aim, eliminating its switching margin.
- The model omits decision history that affects continuation: current aim, commitment, hold time remaining and accumulated spend.

**Cheapest fix sequence:**
1. Log actual `old_aim → new_aim`, reason, predicted improvement, dwell time and reversals.
2. Compute all decisions from one immutable snapshot, then commit them.
3. Track attempt-specific budgets; prevent restarting an exhausted attempt without changed circumstances. Preserve immediate safety exits, but stop discretionary re-entry loops.
4. Add the missing decision-state features and retrain.

Do not start by increasing `hold_s`: that could conceal incorrect state handling. Test iteration-order invariance with breaks off.

## 3. Early speed: weak average value is not yet a defect

**Measured:** the mile estimates are compatible with a small benefit or approximately zero; “every mean positive” is not evidence that every effect is positive.

**H:** with `open_early=0`, most sitters deliberately leave early capability unused; early speed matters mainly in securing position, while later decisions can surrender that gain.

Your “frozen” arm still allows adaptive checks, so it cannot isolate this.

**Small test:** on the existing 20 fields, compare the early-speed upgrade under:
- a fixed, predeclared aim schedule, with learned switching disabled;
- adaptive driving.

For top/pocket attempts versus sitters, separately record first-turn position, acquisition energy, extra ground, absolute finish time and place. Compare learned opening choices against five-aim counterfactual choices for the same upgraded horse.

**Reading:** early speed secures a better position but the adaptive policy loses its benefit → controller problem. Little acquisition benefit even under suitable fixed aims → investigate opening execution. Do not grant race-long efficiency merely to increase the trait’s average value.

## 4. Regret: measure expected-action regret, not hindsight regret alone

Current regret compares the chosen aim with the **best realized arm under one random seed**. That benchmark knows the future; even an optimal expected-value policy can lose against it.

For **20 untouched states**, run every feasible aim over, initially, **20 continuation seeds**:
- Estimate each aim’s mean place.
- Select the best mean aim using one seed subset; evaluate choices on the other, then swap.
- Compare ridge, trees and this sample-based oracle on identical states.
- Increase repetitions where aim differences remain uncertain.

Also estimate the hindsight advantage:

\[
\min_a E[L_a] - E[\min_a L_a].
\]

That is the stochastic contribution to your present metric—not a universal floor for expected-action regret. Missing state features create additional avoidable error.

Use per-horse/time random streams for coupling; equal seeds alone do not guarantee equal shocks after paths diverge. Hold out fresh states never used in fitting **or policy-generation feedback**.
