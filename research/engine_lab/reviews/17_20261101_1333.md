## A. Above-threshold pace: useful, not a sufficient explanation

**Some sustained reserve expenditure is necessary in this model to reward saving energy. A uniformly hard pace is not.** Your results reject constant `lead_push=3` as a complete fix: it mostly transfers wins to first-over while destroying the trait balance.

Three structural issues matter:

- **Soft pace erases the benefit of cover.** Followers cannot bank savings above `e_max`; recovery erases earlier expenditure. With `fade=.3`, a leader at 16/18 retains **96.5% of its ceiling above held speed**. A sitter at 18/18 has little finishing advantage. Increasing recovery would generally worsen this saturation, not solve it.
- **Pressure can perversely lower commanded pace.** `held_speed()` includes duel cost, and the leader commands `held_speed + push + dig`. Thus becoming duelled lowers the base before `dig` is added. At the supplied power scale, that reduction can exceed `dig`; moreover, duel activation requires speed above the *straight-line* `v_sus`, potentially producing on/off cycling. **Your pressure-driven trial is not a clean test of pressure-driven pace.** Log this first.
- **The driver strongly protects incumbents.** Pocket horses must wait until `last_turn_end - 100`; closers deliberately surrender early position; everyone begins the drive at the leader’s trigger. These policies can suppress passing even when followers have usable reserve.

I would distinguish **unpressed threshold**, **actual expenditure under pressure**, and **tactical pace selection**. Don’t automatically lower a defending leader’s requested speed because defending became costly; let reserve depletion and its subsequent decision to yield handle that cost.

## B. Restoring gate and kick without bonuses

**First remove behavioral suppression; then fit the energy budget.**

- **Gate:** `gate_pace=0`, so non-leavers get no gate-dependent settling command. They instead receive lane-dependent takeback, including `gate_drop=5`. More acceleration cannot help a horse whose driver commands deceleration. Let gate ability inform feasible early slots and whether to advance or take back—not an unconditional speed reward.
- **Kick:** determine whether horses actually reach their kick-dependent ceiling, for how long, and whether traffic or depletion prevents using it. At reserve fractions .31/.54, `fade=.3` still preserves roughly **70%/83% of direct ceiling sensitivity to kick**. Near-zero finishing-place value is therefore not explained simply by “the ceiling faded away.”
- **Larger `e_max`:** defensible if the drive is genuinely energy-starved, but it also weakens the relative advantage of saving energy. Test it separately from reducing `p_cruise`. A +.5 cruise increment supplies an additional `.025` power—about **two reserve units over 80 seconds of above-threshold running**, before speed/tactical feedback.
- **Kick-dependent acceleration:** secondary. Add it only if acceleration is binding during meaningful finishing opportunities. The existing acceleration equation already charges for acceleration; retain that accounting.

Prefer pace emerging from leaders’ abilities, challenges, yielding and reserve budgets. Exogenous random hardness is a useful diagnostic, not yet an explanation.
