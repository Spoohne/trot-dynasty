## 1. Before interpreting round 2

It therefore changes both measurement and selection—not just inheritance. Public estimates/tier shares are useful player-facing outcomes, but insufficient controls. **Recommendation:** report season-0 discontinuities, within-variant trends, raw genetic trait distributions, and a frozen common evaluator alongside native scores. Verify that `race_score(4)` really excludes development/training; its implementation is not attached.

Keep relative thresholds for within-population advantage, but add frozen baseline ability thresholds. The pooled midparent slope is descriptive, not a clean inheritance parameter: season, nick and selection can confound it.

## 2. Fitter targets and loss

**Recommendation: treat the current search as exploratory, not acceptance testing.**

- **Targets are hypotheses, not calibration evidence.** Neutral-field style parity is a reasonable provisional fairness objective, but no evidence establishes the exact favorite-win, wire, time or strong-horse bands. “Best” uses a legacy weighted score, so fitting its win rate partly forces agreement with that score. Test graded trait/class contrasts instead.

- **Several scenarios don’t isolate their claimed cause.** **Code findings:** cover versus first-over changes move timing *and* following; early versus waiting also changes following. Scenarios use different horses/posts/random draws. A “cover” instruction does not guarantee actual cover. Use cloned fields, paired interventions and actual trip exposure logs. Cover need not universally improve win probability when it sacrifices position.

- **The loss underweights the stated directional goals.** **Code finding:** zero closer benefit costs only `4 × .02 = .08`; zero cover benefit costs `.04`. A large style imbalance can outweigh both. Ratios also become unstable near zero, while seconds and probabilities have arbitrary relative weights. Use explicit acceptance gates for essential directions, then a standardized soft loss inside those gates.

- **Sampling is too thin for those margins.** At 150 trials per condition, uncertainty in a win-rate difference can readily exceed the requested 1–2 percentage points. Each configuration gets different seeds, inviting selection of lucky fits. Screen on a common scenario bank, then retest finalists with larger samples and untouched seeds; reserve half-mile tracks and other field compositions for validation.

- **Fix mechanics before trusting a fit.** **Code findings:** horses update sequentially, so followers inspect already-moved leaders; neighborhood lists become stale after lane changes; finish times are recorded before traffic resolution; and endpoint-only traffic checks can miss a horse crossing through another. **H:** optimization may compensate for these artifacts rather than calibrate credible racing.

2. **`pkg` still shows positive late-crop ability drift.** Its best-nick advantage over average AI is smaller than `nick3`’s reported advantage because the AI itself breeds better; best-nick still beats ordinary.

5. **`pkg_both` produces the least native-scale drift.** I am less confident it preserves enough absolute breeding reward; that is the main tradeoff to inspect.

## 4. Next experiment I’d prioritize

**A race-mechanics falsification gate before another tuning round.**

Use fixed deterministic two-/three-horse cases for catching, blocking, passing, lane changes and finishing. Compare the current update with snapshot-based simultaneous updates at `DT = .5, .25, .125`; then rerun paired duel and cover scenarios on the same fields.

Measure illegal crossings, finish-order disagreements, time convergence and stability of scenario effects.

**H:** some apparent front-runner advantage and cover failure comes from update/traffic mechanics, not parameter values. This experiment distinguishes those explanations cheaply. If mechanics pass and effects converge, tuning is justified; if not, a lower fitted loss would be misleading.
