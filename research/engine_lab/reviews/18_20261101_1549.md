**Verdict:** The measurements establish post lock-in, not its cause. A–C are plausible directions, but the plan understates how much execution must change. Changing aims and thresholds while retaining controllers that undo those aims would be another symptom patch.

## 1. Start: local judgment or explicit slots?

**Local judgment is reasonable; “quicker than everyone inside” is too restrictive.** Clearing depends on distance, relative speed, yielding, available gaps and reserve—not just gate rankings. The rail horse need not be fastest to defend its position.

You need an explicit **execution state for staying outside and following cover**, with a dynamically chosen target horse/gap. You do not need a centrally assigned slot for every horse.

Currently, “settle in the outer line” does not exist:
- Non-leavers before the turn command `y=RAIL_Y`, with lane-dependent takeback.
- Outside sitters call `slot()` to merge inward.
- Merge deadlock handling slows them behind the horse beside them.
- Parked-budget exhaustion changes their aim again.

Those mechanisms will dismantle A’s intended second line unless changed together.

Also, the claimed one-way yielding is not fully implemented: `crossing` accepts **any outside `top` leaver**, regardless of `ahead_of`. Audit that before relying on the pecking order.

**Keep legitimate “unchallenged leader” behavior.** Removing “found the lead” does not fix how the horse inherited that lead.

## 2. Middle: brush or followers?

**Both are plausible bottlenecks; neither is demonstrated.**

There are concrete restrictions:
- First-over normally waits until `f >= 0.55`.
- Any existing `move` horse can suppress another first-over attempt—even a presser.
- Followers latch only when a mover is already just ahead and outside; closers additionally wait until `f > 0.6`.
- Outside sitters still try to merge inward.
- Everyone enters `drive` when the leader reaches approximately **0.735 mile**. The 3/4 gate therefore already includes drive behavior.

An earlier brush may help, but **“reaches the leader by the 3/4” cannot be a universal physical promise**. Ability, initial deficit, traffic and reserve determine feasibility. Specify a capped, budgeted attempt with abort/settle behavior, not a guaranteed arrival.

Followers need a viable joining and retention policy, not just faster first-over speed. Measure **attempt → successful exit → advance → cover acquired → cover retained**, rather than inferring mechanism from winner trip labels.

Keeping the pace formula does **not** keep the pace distribution or trip mix: more challengers trigger more `dig` and consume more reserve.

## Stage designs likely to repeat the pattern

- **A’s gate is contaminated by B:** half-mile positions reflect both the start and subsequent tactics. Add early clearance/order and two-line occupancy measurements.
- **B’s correlation target can reward pointless churn.** Require physically successful, affordable moves; report uncertainty rather than tuning to 0.8–0.9.
- **C partly redescribes existing behavior:** everyone already drives together at `cap`. The obstacles are lane availability, exit geometry, spacing and remaining energy. “Everyone fans out” is not a feasible simultaneous command, and one passing lane cannot release every rail horse.
- **Closer-win quotas can reward cooking the leaders.** Measure closer opportunity and conversion conditional on pace, deficit and reserve.
- **Deferring all joint evaluation to D is backwards.** Monitor joint outcomes throughout. Otherwise A–C can pass separately and fail together. Safety remains a hard gate; arbitrary outcome bands should not dictate tactical behavior.
