## 1. Highest-priority blockers

### P0 — The half-mile target needs an executable denominator contract

The eight percentages sum to **88.9%, not 100%**. Their source is wins per finished start across variable-sized fields; the spec now labels them “8 starters.” Those are different measurements.

Do not silently normalize the curve or tune toward an impossible exact eight-starter distribution. Preserve Patrick’s selected reference, but explicitly settle how it is scored: source-compatible cohort/denominators, or documented tolerances for the eight-starter check. This is a measurement blocker, not an argument for changing his choice.

Also freeze before tuning:

- Definitions of **favorite**, **rated bottom third**, and **true ability**—not interchangeable rankings.
- Exclusive trip classification based on **realized trajectory**, not intended aim.
- Item 7’s rerun protocol: what stays fixed, and whether posts, condition and random streams change.
- “Photo,” horse length, quarter timing and all tolerances.

The older provisional chart bands must not become competing acceptance targets.

### P0 — Collision avoidance is not yet a guarantee: 0.1–0.3, 0.18, 0.21

A following-speed cap can demand more braking than allowed. Independently clear lateral sweeps can collide when two horses accept moves simultaneously. Endpoint checks can miss between-step contact.

**Fix:**

- Maintain a **braking-safe following invariant**, including a leader breaking stride.
- Resolve simultaneous longitudinal/lateral proposals together, using continuous swept-body checks over the step.
- Reserve the maneuver corridor sufficiently far ahead to make a committed change feasible; check it again during execution.
- Specify conflict resolution, ties and safe fallback without array-order or post-index priority.
- Use the same geometry for replay interpolation and audit.

“Reverse if a body enters the gap” explicitly weakens **0.3**. Prefer safe deceleration or pausing lateral progress while retaining the destination, rather than reversal. The auditor’s “more than two reversals in ten seconds” is also weaker than the rule.

### P0 — Expense cannot enforce a prohibition: 0.4, 0.6, 0.8, 0.21

“Four wide becomes prohibitive” does not ensure it happens only for allowed reasons. Likewise, “takes back” is not an executable gate-sorting policy.

**Fix:** introduce a hard **maneuver-admissibility layer**, separate from tactical preferences:

- Turn-entry predictions: reachable clearance, remaining braking distance and reachable tuck-in gaps.
- Begin yielding before clearance becomes impossible, not when the horse reaches the turn.
- Every four-wide turn occupancy requires a recorded, geometrically verifiable exception.
- Stretch passing corridors permit fanning into clear space but prohibit cutting across a rival’s occupied/reachable corridor.

Use **eight abreast at the gate** as the first integrated feasibility test. Gate speed and initial spacing are crucial with only 300 ft to sort.

## 3. Physics choices I would change

### 1. Reserve: use workload, not only excess speed

The proposed tank is closer to a **finite high-intensity reserve** than an “aerobic tank.”

Use a sustainable power/workload level plus finite reserve:

- Demand includes speed, acceleration, exposure and turn/path effects.
- Cruise improves efficiency/sustainable workload.
- Demand above sustainable capacity spends reserve; recovery below it is bounded and gradual.
- Drafting reduces the aerodynamic component, not every cost.
- Grit discounts the **additional duel cost**, not baseline locomotion.

Currently a high-grit duel can make racing cheaper than being unchallenged. That invites challenge-seeking exploits.

A full physiology simulator is unnecessary; an interpretable power-balance model is sufficient.

### 2. Ceiling: separate the jobs of the traits

`V_top(kick)` governs the whole race, Gate improves acceleration everywhere, and Grit raises top speed. That blurs the decided trait roles.

I would use:

- Gate: launch response and smoothly fading early burst.
- Cruise: efficiency.
- Kick: drive capability.
- Grit: reduced incremental challenge cost.
- Manners: reduced break/line-control risk.

Treat higher capability as **available**, never compulsory. Start with the simplest smooth fatigue curve; do not fit the 0.86 floor before observing actual late fades.

### 3. Coordinates and body size: define physical units first

Specify whether `v` is physical forward speed or `ds/dt`. With physical forward speed:

\[
\dot{s}=v/(1+\kappa y)
\]

and lateral motion also contributes to total ground speed/workload. Ceilings, energy and collision checks must use consistent quantities.

Replace “2.2 lengths” with separate measured feet for:

- horse length used in results;
- horse-plus-sulky footprint;
- safety/headway allowance.

Otherwise body size becomes a hidden tuning knob for pocket trapping, traffic, breaks and margins. Rectangles may suffice, but evaluate them in track/world geometry on turns, not merely as constant boxes in rail coordinates.

### 4. Lateral motion: bound acceleration as well as speed

A lateral speed limit still permits instant starts/stops sideways. Use a smooth maneuver profile, simultaneous swept checks and maneuver feasibility. Avoid reserving excessive empty space: that can make all passing impossible and falsely create front-end bias.

### 5. Break hazard: make it step-invariant and auditable

For intensity \(\lambda\), use:

\[
P(\text{break in step})=1-e^{-\lambda\Delta t}.
\]

Define traffic distances from **body boundaries**, not centers. Integrate exposure, suppress/reset hazard appropriately during recovery, and audit which factor generated risk.

“Near ceiling” should measure chosen effort/load carefully: changing Kick must not create a new hazard feedback accidentally. Calibrate frequency only after traffic and effort are stable; calibrate lost ground separately.

Also restore the spec’s existing **freshness/training inputs**, currently missing from the design.

## 4. Can a rule-based driver plausibly hit Part 1?

**Yes, plausibly; the proposed aim score alone is insufficient.** It needs:

1. **Short-horizon feasibility prediction:** clear, yield, tuck, follow, pass.
2. **Own-horse tactical valuation:** expected position, reserve expenditure, trapping risk and remaining opportunity.
3. **Persistent maneuver state:** commitment, event-triggered replanning and opportunity-specific cooldowns.
4. **Continuous execution controllers:** pace, following gap and lateral trajectory.

Best-response planning is reasonable, but “quickest first” can create first-mover privilege and Gate discontinuities. Test alternative update orders and convergence. All drivers should model rivals, not cooperate to fill target trip shares.

Use short rollouts first as an **offline regret diagnostic**. If a fixed rule repeatedly leaves obvious winning moves unused, improve that rule before adding production search.

Also resolve the wording contradiction: the engine necessarily knows starting geometry, and the driver explicitly uses post. The prohibition should be **no post-specific performance bonuses or target-percentage lookup**, not “nothing knows post.”

## 5. Riskiest couplings and fitting order

**Biggest risk: traits → plan changes → traffic/effort → breaks/fatigue → outcome.** Positive trait coefficients do not protect that chain.

A Gate upgrade can trigger a contested lead attempt; Cruise can prolong an unwinnable duel; Kick can trigger an earlier move. Each can worsen results despite better mechanics.

**Second risk: ability spread versus items 4–7.** These statistics depend heavily on the field population and ranking definitions. Do not fit trait coefficients only to 6–7 while ignoring clocks, favorites and upsets.

For independently rerun races under a fixed field/protocol, winner repeat probability is \(\sum_i p_i^2\). Thus **45% repeat winners is not a free “randomness knob”** alongside 30% wins for the true-best horse. Those targets require a joint feasibility check; they are not automatically contradictory because true-best need not be the most likely winner.

Recommended staging:

1. **Freeze contracts, field generators, inherited trait distributions and condition inputs.**
2. **Freeze geometry, units, bodies and safety.** No target fitting.
3. **Fit isolated capability, workload, fatigue and tier clocks in physical units.** Do not apply a later global time rescale: it changes maneuver and hazard exposure.
4. **Fit tactical competence and Part 0 behavior**, initially without stochastic breaks.
5. **Fit realized trip/post/pace patterns**, holding physics tightly constrained.
6. **Add break calibration, then jointly evaluate 4–7 and monotonicity.**
7. Use small bounded joint adjustments, fresh held-out fields and paired seeds. Reopen a frozen block only with evidence of the failing mechanism.

No artificial noise purely to make item 7 pass.

## 6. What to measure first

Before aggregate win percentages:

1. **Safety:** minimum swept-body separation, required versus available braking, rejected/conflicting moves, replay-quantization error.
2. **Gate sorting:** width at turn entry, clear/tuck time, parked duration and exception reasons.
3. **Single-horse mechanics:** rail versus exposed outside versus cover at matched physical pace and matched rail progress; reserve spent, path distance and fatigue slope.
4. **Canonical tactical cases:** unpressed/pressed leader, failed lead bid, blocked pocket, moving cover, stretch fan and break in a packed line.
5. **Trait counterfactuals:** paired upgrades under identical commands, then adaptive policies; log the *first decision divergence* and its downstream cost.
6. **Only then aggregate outcomes**, with uncertainty and conditioning by track, field size, post, ability, realized trip and break status.

**Build recommendation:** move the replay adapter and exact-trajectory audit forward into steps 1–2. Safety, physics and what Patrick sees must share one representation from the outset—not be reconciled after tuning.
