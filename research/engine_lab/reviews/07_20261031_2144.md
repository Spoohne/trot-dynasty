## 1. H10: plausible, but the latest test does not isolate it

**Code finding:** increasing `tank_full` from 45 to 100 makes fatigue start earlier **but weakens its sensitivity to energy**. For an average horse, below the threshold:

- At 45: speed changes by **0.168 ft/s per energy point**.
- At 100: only **0.0756 ft/s per point**.

A 20-point energy advantage therefore buys about **3.36 versus 1.51 ft/s**, when both horses are in that linear region. The observed small differential is partly built into the intervention. This did not test “freshness matters more”; it tested earlier, shallower fatigue.

Two additional **code findings**:
- `budget_speed` still uses the **old expenditure formula**, even with `split` enabled, and applies only to `h.moving`—typically not the leader going for home under `drive_all`. Disable it for the mechanism test.
- The split branch does not itself subtract `air_k` from baseline cost. Confirm that the experiment configuration performed the normalization described in item 75.

**Conclusion:** H10 remains a useful hypothesis about the achievable speed trajectory, not a demonstrated need for higher maximum speed. Unspent energy alone does not establish a defect: finite ability can legitimately limit expenditure. Patrick’s earlier-move hypothesis deserves testing before further physiology changes.

## 2. Physically grounded direction

**Recommendation / hypothesis:** distinguish **maximum speed**, **sustainable effort**, and **finite reserve for effort above sustainable**.

A horse may reach its trait-defined maximum while fresh, but cannot hold that speed indefinitely if required power exceeds sustainable output. Cover reduces required power; reserve funds the excess. Depletion reduces sustainable performance. Apply this identically to every horse.

That is preferable to guaranteeing enough late speed to erase seven lengths. Some deficits should be unrecoverable.

For the present model, a smaller intervention is a **steeper fatigue loss concentrated at low reserve**, retaining the existing fresh maximum. Specify the actual speed–energy curve rather than calling it “convex”: curvature alone does not determine the useful speed difference.

Do **not** simultaneously raise the ceiling, steepen costs and change pacing. Also, running slower early creates a larger deficit; it helps only if the saved reserve demonstrably pays for more recovery later.
