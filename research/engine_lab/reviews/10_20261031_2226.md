## Astra

## 1. Reject the “isolated physics problem” and “90% monotonicity” readings

**Code finding:** `ghost + clear_path` is **not alone**. The subject still:
- tracks the live leader;
- checks live rivals when tucking in;
- receives shelter from real horses;
- can influence leader selection and other drivers’ decisions.

Item 96’s “couldn’t tuck because of rivals” demonstrates that remaining interaction. Call this **collision-disabled**, not isolated.

Also, frozen plans freeze `leave`, `move_at` and desired gap—not the speed requests or realized trip. Better early speed still changes acceleration, requested pace and subsequent interactions. Thus item 93 does not isolate physiology from driving.

Finally, **“faster” and “not worse place” are different metrics**. Item 97’s 90% is place non-degradation; item 98 treats it as time monotonicity. Reject that substitution and the arbitrary “every trait helps in ≥85%” gate.

## 2. The fixes have different levels of justification

- **`open_early=0`: provisionally defensible as a driver-policy change.** More capability need not force a sitter to spend it immediately. It is not a physical law, and leavers/other phases still request more speed as early speed rises.
- **`ease_early`: physically possible, but currently a trait-definition change—not an established correction.** Better locomotor efficiency could raise an economical pace. But your documented early-speed trait means gate speed/acceleration; this adds **race-long efficiency**, overlapping stamina and explaining why early speed becomes so valuable.

**Code finding:** the modified `over` also feeds break pressure. `ease_early` therefore improves both energy economy **and gait reliability** at a given speed. That effect is absent from the breaks-off probe but present in the profile assay.

**H:** you may be compensating for an overaggressive controller by granting extra efficiency. Do not select `ease_early` merely because it crosses a monotonicity percentage. If retained, define its intended physiological meaning and decouple its break effect unless that coupling is deliberate.

## 3. The remaining 19% is unresolved—not automatically acceptable variance

These runs are deterministic. Call it **interaction sensitivity**, not random trip variance.

A stronger horse can legitimately obtain a worse trip, but a planner that systematically chooses inferior tactics after an upgrade is still defective. The aggregate 19% cannot distinguish those cases.

**Smallest next test, using the existing fields:**

For upgraded early speed, compare:
1. All baseline plans frozen.
2. Only the subject replanned.
3. Everyone replanned.

Record absolute time and place separately, plan changes, extra ground and obstruction losses. Trace the cases where arm 2 loses materially to arm 1; rerun those at DT 1/32.

Separately, replace the “alone” diagnostic with **one runner and a scripted absolute pace request**, without field-dependent driving. Replay the same feasible speed/path schedule before and after the upgrade; measure expenditure. Then compare attainable finish times over the **same small menu of pacing schedules**. This distinguishes capability from automatically spending the upgrade badly.

Use uncertainty clustered by the **20 fields**, not 160 supposedly independent subjects.

## 4. Do not rebalance trait weights yet

**Accept H13 only narrowly:** at this configuration and intervention size, the reported mean time gain from early speed is roughly three times that from late speed. That is not a universal exchange rate, and 46% of wins alone does not establish unacceptable imbalance.

**Recommended order:**
1. Repair the diagnostic labels and isolate policy versus capability.
2. Decide whether early speed genuinely includes sustained economy.
3. Complete continuous lateral motion and collision validation.
4. Then perform the narrow, held-out trait-sensitivity fit across traits, posts, pace scenarios and venues.

I would reject:
- tuning `kick_late` upward solely to cancel an unvalidated `ease_early`;
- requiring every deterministic upgrade to improve every finish;
- accepting 19% reversals without explaining their causes;
- calling these two changes physically validated “fixes.”

**Bottom line:** retain `open_early=0` as a plausible policy candidate; keep `ease_early` experimental until the truly isolated test establishes what problem it is solving.
