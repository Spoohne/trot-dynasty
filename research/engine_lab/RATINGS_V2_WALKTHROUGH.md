# Ratings v2: the walkthrough

Open the candidate at http://127.0.0.1:8113 (a copy of the live world run forward seven race nights, signed in as your
barn, never saving) beside trotdynasty.com. The world is the same; what differs is how it's read.

## 1. The letter

Every horse's letter (S+ … D) now comes from how it has run, not from a hidden rating. A proven horse (five or more
starts) wears the median of its last ten figures; a horse with fewer than five starts wears a hollow letter marked
UNPROVEN, read off its bars against horses its age; a retired horse keeps its prime. Under a proven horse's name:
"Figure 1:53.2 · its last ten starts" is what the letter rests on.

Look at: Saga Spell (10, in decline, A on the candidate), Twin Bronco, Northern Star, your own barn.

## 2. Figures

Each start on a horse page shows the clock and, under it, "fig 1:50.8": what the horse ran on its own account, the trip
it got taken out. A horse towed home behind a fast winner shows a quick clock and a modest figure; one that did the work
parked outside gets credit. A break leaves no figure (—). The program's past-performance lines carry a Fig column; the
Results page shows the figure beside each clock.

## 3. Form, after the fact

Trip notes end "sharp tonight" or "flat tonight" when the night's form was big either way. An upset reads as what it was.

## 4. The replay

A horse that breaks stride hops for a few seconds where it happened, and the caption lands on it: "X breaks stride". A
sharp winner's caption says so. Positions are the choreographer's, as live.

## 5. The Compendium

More → Compendium → Racing → Figures explains the above in plain words, no numbers.

## What the numbers say (research/engine_lab/RATINGS_V2.md)

On the live world, S+ is 3.9% of active horses under v2 against 10.7% under live. Over 100 fresh seasons the candidate's
letter agrees with the engine's own reading of the horse 77% of the time (live 45%) and flips less (34% vs 52%). Live's
letter predicts next season's purses a little better for horses 4 and up (.74 vs .71), because it also carries who the
horse beat. The choice: a letter that says how fast the horse is (v2) or how it fares (live).
