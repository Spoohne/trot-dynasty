Engine Review

Third version. Every finding comes from simulation on a copy of the live world; the live game is unchanged. 48 independent 50-season histories (6 variants × 8 seeds, matched seeds across variants), about 300,000 recorded births, plus a training test on cloned horses and a prototype step-by-step race engine.

Answers to the three questions:

Do informed breeding choices improve outcomes? Yes, already. Matching bloodlines (nicks) beats average AI breeding by about eight rating points per foal under today's rules. Strengthening nicks doubles that edge.

Do management and tactics matter? Barely, today. Six training programs on identical horses produce near-identical race results. The race formula has no tactics to choose.

Does the population stay varied? Partly. Running styles and sibling variety hold steady. Sires do not: five stallions sire about 73% of racers in every history.

Corrections to the second version.

Foal potential isn't flat. Over 50 seasons it rises slowly, and comparing crops at the same age shows that rise accounts for all of the rating gain. Inflation is genetic, from steady selection, not from development or retirement. The 30-season, two-seed runs were too short to see it.

Nicks aren't negligible. A nick moves each trait only a little, but every trait at once, so the total is large. I understated it.

Strategic breeders do gain. The earlier runs measured whole racing stables, which mixes in bought and old horses. Measured foal by foal, informed breeding wins.

Definitions

Potential
A horse's fixed genetic peak: its race score at age 4 from its inherited genes, before any training or racing. One point of potential is about six rating points.

True rating
The engine's internal 0–99 measure of a horse today. It counts unfinished development in full, even though races count only a small share of it, so it isn't used here to judge ability.

Public estimate
What the game shows players, learned from results. Tiers (S, A, B…) are graded from it on a fixed scale of projected mile times.

Midparent
The average potential of a foal's sire and dam.

Inheritance slope
How much a foal's potential rises per point of midparent potential, across all births. 1 would mean foals average their parents; 0 would mean parents don't matter.

Useful / exceptional
A foal above the median potential of all births in its variant / in the top 5%.

The variants

Each variant changes one rule; "combined" changes all four. Every variant also has 12 strategy barns with the same $6M budget, two for each plan: top sire, speed, cover the mare's weakness, best nick, close breeding, and ordinary (a random affordable sire).

Today's rules inflate slowly and steadily. About one rating point per decade, still rising at season 50, though the rise is slowing. The comparison at age 4 shows it is genetic.

Every change that makes breeding more effective also speeds inflation. Strong heredity alone reaches 36% S or S+. All four changes together reach 49%, which is runaway.

The cause is consistent selection. AI and players breed the best to the best every season. Nothing in the rules pulls the herd back except genes resetting to average, and that is too weak to hold the line once breeding improves.

Informed breeding already pays, and nicks are the best lever. Today the best-nick plan beats average AI foals by 1.4 potential, about eight rating points. Strong nicks more than double that, and widen the gap from ordinary breeders. That's your "find the best fit for your best" skill.

"Best to best" works but leaves room for luck. The best fifth of pairings gives useful foals 55% of the time against 45% for the worst fifth. Exceptional foals stay rare everywhere, about 5%, and full siblings vary just as much under every rule. Breeding is never solved.

Spreading mares narrows the edge. When the AI breeds smarter, the average AI foal improves, so every player plan gains less relative to it.

How the AI picks mates is the main cause. Spreading AI mares alone cuts the top-five share from 73% to about 50%. Small books alone cut it to 66%, and they also halve how related the leading sires are.

Sire lines collapsed long ago. In every variant, more than 99% of racers trace their top sire line to three founders. That happened in the world's simulated history, before launch. Because traits are spread over many genes, it doesn't limit variety the way a single gene would, but it means "outside blood" isn't a story the game can tell yet.

It also means the internal true rating can't be used as an ability measure.

The race engine

Today's engine

One formula gives each horse a time, the trip is a text label, and the replay builds positions afterward. That's confirmed in the code. The rating favorite wins about 56% of real races in every variant, and the result in a close field comes down to one random roll.

The prototype

A separate module, not used by the game, runs the mile in half-second steps. Each horse has a position, lane, speed and energy. Drivers choose moves from style, instructions and traffic, and a horse can't pass through the horse in front. The finish order is whoever reaches the wire first, and every race returns a call-by-call record a replay can draw directly.

The structure works: decisions and traffic change outcomes for reasons a replay can show. Its calibration doesn't. I stopped hand-tuning after five rounds. The next step is an automated fitting loop that tunes about a dozen settings toward style balance, closer viability and the scenario directions above.

Recommendations

1. Spread AI mares
The strongest single fix for sire concentration, from 73% to about 50%. Pair it with recommendation 4, because a smarter AI breeds the herd up faster.

2. Books of 30
A modest diversity gain, and it halves relatedness among the leading sires. It also limits stud income for the economy work.

3. Stronger nicks, about double
The cleanest skill lever: the best-nick plan's edge grows most, ordinary breeders fall behind, and inflation barely moves on its own. Test double, not triple, together with 1 and 2.

Don't raise heredity yet
Heredity drove the fastest inflation of any single change, and it added little skill edge beyond what nicks give.

4. A stable counterweight to inflation
Not a secret nerf, and not a cap on any horse. Candidates to test openly: stronger diminishing returns at the top of each trait, and slightly more genes resetting toward the breed average. Keep absolute ability tracked, and show players both an ability measure and their current standing.

5. Make training count
Let unfinished development show more on the track for young horses, so a well-trained 2- or 3-year-old races closer to its potential sooner. That fits "reach potential faster", not "exceed it".

6. Fit the race prototype automatically
Tune it toward balanced styles, viable closers and sensible scenario directions, then re-run the breeding tests on it, since the traits that win races decide which breeding pays.

Limits: strategy barns don't price stallions, trade or race differently from the AI; stud-fee income wasn't measured; "useful" and "exceptional" are relative to each variant's own births; the race prototype is uncalibrated and runs outside the game.
