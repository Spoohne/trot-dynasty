# Monetization models and benchmarks for mobile management/simulation games (2024–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-30. Figures are dated inline. Caveat up front: no public source gives ARPDAU, ARPPU or payer-conversion specifically for *management/sports-manager* sims from small studios; the closest public data is genre-level "Simulation" and "Strategy" from GameAnalytics, AppMagic and Appodeal, plus vendor blogs. Treat vendor-blog ranges as directional.

## Key Question 1: What do simulation/management games earn per active user, and what share of players pay?

### Takeaway
Public per-user revenue data for Simulation is thin and mostly indirect. The best-sourced numbers: Simulation is a ~$4.8B/yr IAP genre growing ~11% (AppMagic, Dec 2025), median Simulation D1 retention is ~17% and D28 ~0.5% (GameAnalytics, 2024 data), and payer conversion across mobile games as a whole runs roughly 1–3% of installs, with 3–7% monthly cited as an older industry rule of thumb. Nobody publishes a clean "simulation ARPDAU"; vendor ranges put casual at $0.05–0.15 and mid-core/strategy at $0.08–0.50+.

### Cited Findings
- Simulation worldwide IAP revenue (App Store + Google Play) grew from $4.4B (2024) to $4.8B (2025, through Oct 22), +11.2%; Strategy $10.8B → $13.5B (+25.6%); RPG fell 15.4%; total market $55.2B → $57.1B. Simulation on Google Play $1.59B → $1.64B, App Store $2.8B → $3.2B. Published Dec 4, 2025. — [AppMagic Mobile Games Monetization Report 2025 (via GameDev Reports)](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)
- Simulation subgenres 2025: sandbox +26.9% ($1.1B → $1.4B), work sims +107%, animal sims +106%, fishing −40%. — [AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)
- Simulation ARPPU (US top-10 grossers, YoY 2025): Google Play down up to 7%, App Store up ~10%; purchases per payer down up to 6% (GP) and up to 28% (App Store); average price point up 27% (GP) and up to 52% (App Store). Absolute ARPPU not published. — [AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)
- AppMagic counts Simulation as #3 casual IAP category at ~$1.1B in H1 2025 (+5% YoY); only 1 of 55 new farm sims launched in 2025 passed $100k monthly net revenue. — [AppMagic Mobile Casual Games H1'25 via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-casual-games-in-h125?open=false)
- GameAnalytics 2025 Mobile Gaming Benchmarks (data Jan–Dec 2024, ~11,600 games, 1.48B avg MAU, 16 genres), median (P50) Simulation: D1 16.8–17.3%, D7 2.2–2.6%, D28 0.45–0.53%, daily playtime 14–15 min, 3.6–3.7 sessions/day. Strategy: D1 19–20.7%, D7 3.2–3.6%, D28 0.66–0.77%, playtime 22–25 min. Sports: D1 18.8–19.7%, D7 3.3–3.9%, D28 0.72–0.89%. Global top-25% games: D1 ~27%, D7 7–8%, D28 2.6–3%. The report does NOT break out ARPDAU/ARPPU/conversion by genre. — [GameAnalytics 2025 Mobile Gaming Benchmarks](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)
- GameAnalytics 2026 benchmarks (calendar 2025, 16,000+ games): median game ~22% D1, under 4% D7, 0.7–0.8% D30 (secondary summary). — [search summary citing GameAnalytics](https://gameanalytics.com/gameintel)
- Earlier GameAnalytics genre report: RPG and Strategy ARPDAU and conversion are 5–7x higher than other genres (report date unclear). — [GameAnalytics genre benchmark (via Mobile Marketing Magazine)](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/?p=113139)
- Appodeal Mobile Casual Benchmarks 2025 (US Android users, Jun 2024–Jan 2025, 10,000+ games, published May 2025): hypercasual ARPU $0.86, Match $2.99, Party $4.90; Merge-3 ad ARPU $14.83. Simulation not broken out. — [App Developer Magazine on Appodeal report](https://appdevelopermagazine.com/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025/)
- Appodeal 2025 (search summary): hypercasual and simulation lead downloads but suffer ~3% 30-day retention and ARPU ~$0.79 (US). — [Game Industry Library listing](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025)
- Vendor ARPDAU ranges (2025–2026, conflicting): hardcore/strategy $0.30–1.50+ and mid-core $0.15–0.40 ([Adapty](https://adapty.io/glossary/arpdau/)); mid-core $0.08–0.25, top $0.30–0.50 ([AdReact](https://adreact.com/blog/arpdau-calculator-benchmark-guide/)); casual $0.05–0.15, hypercasual $0.02–0.10 across most sources; RewardingHub says multiply US/Canada figures by 0.6–0.8 for Western Europe ([RewardingHub](https://business.rewardinghub.com/blog/monetization-benchmarks-by-genre/)). Playio and Galaxy4Games note there is no published primary ARPDAU benchmark for IAP-driven mid-core, ~90% of whose revenue is IAP. — [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/arpdau-benchmarks-mobile-games); [Galaxy4Games](https://galaxy4games.com/en/knowledgebase/blog/mobile-game-monetization-trends-2026-what-publishers-and-studios-should-track)
- D90 IAP ARPPU for mobile games roughly $7–10 (secondary blog, Oct 2026). — [Playio ARPPU post](https://blog.playio.co/arppu-mobile-games)
- Payer conversion: ~0.8% of mobile game users make an IAP (Gitnux compilation attributing Adjust); iOS users convert ~1.25x Android. — [Gitnux 2026](https://gitnux.org/mobile-games-statistics/)
- Midcore payer-conversion genre benchmark ~3% within first 14 days (vendor page, unverified). — [Bruin](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/midcore-payer-conversion-rate-vs-genre-benchmark/)
- Older industry rule of thumb: 3–7% monthly conversion, with some audiences reaching ~20% lifetime conversion (undated). — [Gamesbrief](https://gamesbrief.com/?p=7558)
- Strategy ARPPU on App Store historically 4–5x Google Play, widening to up to 8x in 2025 at some lifecycle stages. — [AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)
- Overall market: mobile game revenue ~$82B in 2025 (+~1% YoY, Sensor Tower), with growth almost entirely captured by the top 50 games. — [Sensor Tower State of Mobile Gaming 2025](https://sensortower.com/state-of-mobile-gaming-2025); [Deconstructor of Fun analysis](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/seven-things-the-2025-state-of-gaming-report-actually-tells-us)
- Only ~2,500 of ~190,000 mobile games released in 2025 passed 500,000 downloads. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/report-only-2500-of-190000-mobile-games-released-in-2025-surpassed-500000-downloads/)

### Inferences
- A small management sim should plan around median Simulation retention (~17% D1, ~0.5% D28) unless it has a strong social/competitive loop; Strategy and Sports medians are meaningfully better and a sports-manager game likely sits between Simulation and Strategy.
- With ~1–3% of installs paying and vendor ARPDAU for mid-core at roughly $0.10–0.40, a plausible monthly revenue per active user for a small-studio manager sim is on the order of $1–5 per MAU (my arithmetic from the ranges above, not a sourced figure).
- iOS monetizes dramatically better than Android in strategy-type games (4–8x ARPPU), so platform mix matters more than genre.

### Gaps
- No public absolute ARPDAU, ARPPU or payer % specific to Simulation or sports-manager games in 2024–2026; GameAnalytics' genre chapter only covers retention/engagement publicly.
- No small-studio/indie dataset for mobile sims; AppMagic's payer behaviour covers only US top-10 grossers per genre.
- Liftoff/AppsFlyer 2025 benchmark reports were not found in a form that gives genre-level monetization numbers.

## Key Question 2: How do season passes and cosmetic-only models perform in management/sim genres versus cash-pack models?

### Takeaway
Hard evidence is weak for the sim genre specifically. The directional picture: battle passes are near-universal in top-grossing games (86% of top-100 grossers used one in GameRefinery's count) and contribute anywhere from 1% to ~40% of revenue depending on genre; cosmetic-only games (Fortnite, Marvel Rivals) can gross enormously but rely on scale, while pay-to-win games (Diablo Immortal) earn heavily early then see per-payer revenue decay. Google's new fee tiers now explicitly reward cosmetic-only (9%) over gameplay-advantage (20%) purchases. Top Eleven, the leading mobile football manager, uses tokens packs plus a seasonal battle pass.

### Cited Findings
- Battle pass adoption among top-100 grossing mobile games rose from 74% to 86%, and premium-pass offerings doubled year over year (GameRefinery via TheGamer; date not shown in result, likely 2021–2022). — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/battle-pass-utilization-doubled-past-year/)
- Google reported premium battle passes drove 1–40% of revenue in top games (2021). — [Google Play Apps & Games](https://goo.gle/3htI67y)
- A 2026 compilation claims an 18.4% free-to-play pass conversion, 34.2% midcore mobile revenue share from passes, 18.2% in mobile casual, 28.5% in sports/racing, and a mobile pass price floor of $4.99–6.99; but its citations link only to vendor homepages and its tables don't sum to 100%, so these are unverified. — [VoxBooster battle pass statistics 2026](https://voxbooster.com/blog/battle-pass-statistics-2026/)
- Google Play proposed fee tiers (Nov 2025): 20% for purchases giving more than a de minimis gameplay advantage (loot boxes, boosters, gacha, power items), 9% for cosmetics, extra levels and most subscriptions; +5% if using Google Play Billing. Hybrid cases (battle passes, dual-use currency, starter packs) are unclassified grey areas; bundles default to 20% unless pro-rated. Sensor Tower: 37 of the top 50 US Play grossers in Oct 2025 had loot boxes and nearly all would face the 20% tier; cosmetic-centric games like Fortnite fall in the 9% bracket. — [Outlook Respawn citing Sensor Tower](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/top-50-mobile-games-may-face-20-fee-under-new-play-store-rules); [The Verge via SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/mobile/googles-settlement-with-epic-games-may-lead-to-changes-for-android-devs/)
- Diablo Immortal (pay-to-win example): launched June 2022 to the lowest Metacritic user score of all time yet earned >$24M in its first two weeks; monthly revenue later fell from a peak above $70M (Aug 2022) to ~$6–7M/month in 2026 (AppMagic), with a 41% drop in revenue per paying user 2022–2024 (Sensor Tower). Secondary compilation; 2023–24 figures interpolated. — [ShaneTheGamer research](https://www.shanethegamer.com/research/diablo-immortal/); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/record-diablo-immortal-revenues-shows-grim-future-video-games-blizzard-thrives-pay-to-win-features)
- Marvel Rivals (cosmetic-only example): ~$135M revenue in its first month with no pay-to-win mechanics; a $25 Battle Pass Accelerator in Season 2 triggered pay-to-win accusations and was later rolled back. No reliable data found on a revenue decline afterward. — [FandomWire on $135M](https://fandomwire.com/hype-totally-gone-a-greedy-netease-move-has-shot-marvel-rivals-in-the-foot/); [FandomWire on rollback](https://fandomwire.com/marvel-rivals-has-quietly-rolled-back-its-most-controversial-feature/); [Gfinity on Psylocke gacha](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/gacha-in-marvel-rivals-why-the-psylocke-skin-event-is-a-worrying-sign)
- Cosmetics-only model definition and trade-off: "doesn't capture as much whale spend but preserves community." — [MWM glossary](https://mwm.ai/glossary/pay-to-win)
- Top Eleven (Nordeus, football manager): free with optional IAP including random items; IAP list is token packs and "Special Packages" at 10 price tiers (Qatar storefront: QAR 7.99 to QAR 399.99, i.e. roughly $2–$110), plus a seasonal "Special Sponsor Battle Pass" with milestone rewards (price not listed). — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/top-eleven-be-football-manager/id459035295); [Microsoft Store listing](https://microsoft.com/en-us/p/top-eleven-2022-be-a-soccer-manager/9n2ckl7x976c)
- Older Top Eleven monetization analysis (2016): sells TV/sponsorship deals and tokens; "isn't the most generous" to non-payers but playable free. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/a-new-season-the-monetisation-of-top-eleven)
- Indie ad-led data point (Silly Walks, Part Time Monkey): ads ~97% of revenue, IAP the rest, top IAP item 35% of IAP revenue; $270k total. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/finnish-indie-part-time-monkey-makes-270k-from-silly-walks)
- Indie sim postmortem (Indoorlands, Pixelsplit, PC/Steam, Oct 2023): ~9,000 copies lifetime, ~€100k net, ~€200k production cost, not recouped; Supporter DLC ~$12k net was the best-return item; "indoor theme park" lacked a hook. — [WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/zh/news/analytics/item-42548)
- Vendor claim: an indie studio lifted revenue 20% by adding rewarded ads for currency alongside IAP. — [iXie case study (vendor)](https://ixiegaming.com/successstories/iXie-boosts-revenue-by-20percent-for-mobile.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a management sim with a competitive ladder, the industry pattern is hybrid: a seasonal pass (~$5–10) as the retention/attach anchor, currency packs for whales, and rewarded ads for non-payers. Cosmetic-only alone is viable mainly at Fortnite-scale audiences; small studios rarely publish cosmetic-only success data.
- Google's 9%/20% split (if approved) creates a concrete fee incentive for cosmetic and subscription SKUs over power items; dual-use currency risks being taxed at 20%.
- The Diablo Immortal arc (huge launch, steep ARPPU decay, reputational damage) is the clearest public P2W cautionary tale; the Marvel Rivals accelerator rollback shows even cosmetic games get punished when a pass touches progression.

### Gaps
- No pass attach-rate benchmark from a primary source (Sensor Tower, GameRefinery, GameAnalytics) for 2024–2026 was found; the VoxBooster numbers are unverifiable.
- No Deconstructor of Fun teardown of a sports-manager game was found; the site has only a Football Manager podcast segment.
- No cosmetic revenue share figure for simulation/management titles specifically.
- Top Eleven battle pass price and attach rate not published.

## Key Question 3: Typical price points for a season pass and small premium-currency packs in this genre

### Takeaway
Season/battle passes cluster at $4.99 (mobile/monthly) to $9.99 (two-month, cross-platform), with $24.99–39.99 premium tiers; small currency packs and starter bundles cluster at $0.99–$9.99, while top-grossing strategy games lean on $15+ average transactions and $99 offers.

### Cited Findings
- Diablo Immortal: $4.99 monthly season pass. Overwatch 2: $9.99 two-month pass; Valorant ~$10. Disney Speedstorm Golden Pass $9.99 direct purchase only. Overwatch 2 starter pack $9.99 (1,000 coins + skin). MultiVersus pass 300 Gleamium ≈ $4.99. — [PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/new-world/season-pass-price); [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/esports/guide/overwatch-2-season-5-battle-pass-price-duration-skins-items-more); [VGC on Speedstorm](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/disney-speedstorms-next-season-pass-can-only-be-purchased-with-real-money-not-earned); [TheGamer on MultiVersus](https://www.thegamer.com/multiversus-first-battle-pass-tiers-gleamium-cost/)
- Unverified compilation: standard premium pass $9.99 in 84% of surveyed PC/console titles; mobile mini/event pass $4.99; mobile floor $4.99–6.99; ultra tier $29.99–39.99; pass + 20–25 tier skips $24.99; median season length 77 days. — [VoxBooster](https://voxbooster.com/blog/battle-pass-statistics-2026/)
- AppMagic 2025 price points: Strategy average transaction ~$7 on Google Play and often >$15 on App Store, with $99 offers common among top earners; RPG average first purchase $8.30 (GP) / $17.90 (App Store); event bundles $0.99–4.99, with a $2.99 bundle generating 16% of purchases in Zenless Zone Zero; puzzle LiveOps offers mostly $6–15. — [AppMagic via GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)
- Top Eleven token packs span 10 tiers from ~$2 to ~$110 equivalent (QAR 7.99–399.99), including 80-token and 176-token mid packs. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/top-eleven-be-football-manager/id459035295)

### Inferences
- For a small-studio manager sim: a $4.99 monthly or $9.99 ~2-month pass, an entry currency pack at $0.99–2.99, a $4.99–9.99 starter/value bundle, and a $19.99–49.99 whale tier matches the observed ladders; the AppMagic data suggests the smallest bundle drives the most transactions.

### Gaps
- No 2025 mobile-specific survey of starter-pack or currency-pack prices in Simulation; price points above come from adjacent genres and individual titles.

## Key Question 4: State of web-shop / direct payment for mobile games and realistic fee savings

### Takeaway
As of mid/late 2026, US iOS developers may link out to web payment with zero Apple commission pending a court-set "reasonable" fee; US Android link-outs are effectively fee-free under the Epic v. Google injunction (Google lists link-out fees but says it is not assessing them, with a July 2026 hearing pending). Web shop platforms charge roughly 5% all-in versus 15–30% in-store, so realistic savings are ~20–25 points on the share of revenue moved; mature publishers route 20–40% of revenue through web shops, and 72% of top-grossing mobile games run one.

### Cited Findings
- App store base fees: Apple 30% standard (15% small-business tier exists but the RevenueCat page cites 15–30%); Google historically 30%. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy)
- Epic v. Apple, April 30, 2025 (N.D. Cal.): Apple may not charge a commission on external purchases, restrict link placement/design, or use scare screens beyond a neutral notice; most apps must still also offer IAP (May 3, 2025 update). — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy); [Neon on April 2025 ruling](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/making-sense-of-the-april-30th-2025-epic-v-apple-ruling)
- Ninth Circuit, December 11, 2025: upheld the contempt finding (Apple's 27% linked-out commission and restrictive links violated the injunction), but reversed the blanket ban on any fee; Apple may charge a "reasonable, non-prohibitive" cost-based commission plus limited IP compensation, to be set by the district court (possibly via a Technical Committee). Apple may stop external links being *more* prominent than IAP but must allow equal prominence. Until a rate is approved, US external payments carry zero Apple commission (a "free window"). Court noted 27% + >3% processing exceeded the 30% in-app cost. — [Neon on December 2025 appeal](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/the-latest-epic-v.-apple-appeal-ruling-december-2025-what-changed-for-game-developers)
- Epic v. Google settlement proposal (Nov 2025): service fee capped at 9% (cosmetics, extra levels, most subscriptions, non-game) or 20% (more-than-de-minimis gameplay advantage) for apps using alternative payments, +5% if using Google Play Billing; applies to apps installed/updated after Oct 30; global. Not yet approved by Judge Donato as of 2026; the Oct 2024 permanent injunction remains in force. — [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/mobile/googles-settlement-with-epic-games-may-lead-to-changes-for-android-devs/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/11/05/google-make-major-changes-app-store-settle-epic-games-lawsuit/); [Neon on Google settlement](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/but-what-does-the-google-settlement-actually-mean)
- Google "expanded billing choice and lower fees" (announced June 24, 2026, rollout June 30 in US/EEA/UK): service fee 10% on first $1M annual earnings and on all auto-renewing subscriptions, 20% above; billing fee 5% only when using Google Play Billing (so ~25% typical instead of 30%). Link-out fees (10%/20% plus $2.85–3.65 install-type fees) are listed for the US but Google says it is "not assessing these fees" at this time; Stash argues the Epic v. Google injunction prevents US link-out fees and advises US Android devs to keep zero-fee link-outs; EU/UK devs should expect the fee. July 16, 2026 evidentiary hearing pending. Out-of-app DTC costs ~5% payment processing. — [Stash, June 30, 2026](https://www.stash.gg/blog/googles-lower-fees-announcement-june-2026-whats-really-going-on-and-what-us-developers-should-do)
- Google's own external-offers program pages list 10% for auto-renewing subscriptions and 20% for other content, 10% within the first $1M annual earnings, plus an install fee for external content links (search summary, not fetched directly). — [Google Play Console help via search](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/google-just-announced-new-play-store-fees-heres-whats-actually-happening--how-it-impacts-dtc)
- Web shop platform fees: Xsolla Web Shop launched at 5% per transaction (Dec 2021; verify current pricing). — [BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20211215005807/en/5116073/Mobile-Game-Developers---Take-Full-Control-of-Your-Game.-Xsolla-Introduces-Web-Shop-for-Mobile-Games)
- Xsolla Q2 2025 State of Play (secondary summary): developers can keep as much as 95% of spend on web vs losing 30% to platform fees; projects $126B 2025 mobile game spend. The BusinessWire release returned 403, so the 95% figure is from a secondary summary. — [Outlook Respawn on Xsolla](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/xsolla-predicts-mobile-game-spending-to-reach-126-billion-in-2025)
- Konvoy (Sept 12, 2025): 72% of top-grossing mobile games run their own DTC storefront; publishers from Playtika to Stillfront see up to 40% of revenue through web shops; public gaming companies report 20–40% of revenue from direct sales. — [Konvoy Game Changers newsletter](https://podcast.app/newsletter-mobile-web-shops-the-great-platform-unbundling-e425042000)
- Appcharge: processes >$500M/yr in DTC transactions, 500+ payment methods; recommends in-game promotion of the web store, product deep links landing signed-in, and iOS payment links. Puts the DTC market at ~$17B. — [Appcharge](https://www.appcharge.com/blog/the-ruling-weve-been-waiting-for); [GamesIndustry.biz via GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/topic/web-stores/)
- External-payment share benchmark: ~20–30% of gross mobile game revenue for the savviest teams, with some expecting 50% reachable (vendor blog, 2025). — [Neon on April 2025 ruling](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/making-sense-of-the-april-30th-2025-epic-v-apple-ruling)
- RevenueCat guidance: test modestly lower web prices (e.g. $9.99 → $8.99/$9.49), coupons, better annual pricing; no conversion data published. — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/apple-anti-steering-ruling-monetization-strategy)
- Apple updated US guidelines after the April 2025 ruling to permit external links/buttons without the prior 27% fee. — [Shibolet law firm summary](https://www.shibolet.com/en/apple-guidelines-update-redirecting-users-to-external-payment-mechanism/); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/apple-removes-app-store-payment-restrictions-in-the-us/)

### Inferences
- Realistic all-in web cost for a small studio is ~5% (Stripe-class processing or Xsolla/Stash/Appcharge-style platform fee), versus 15% (Apple small-business) to 30% in-store; net savings ~10–25 points per dollar moved, times the share of payers who shift (20–40% at big publishers, likely lower for an indie without a web-store marketing loop).
- The US "free window" on both stores is legally live but contingent: Apple's fee is pending a district-court rate, and Google's link-out fees exist on paper. Build web payment now but model a 10–20% platform fee returning.
- A browser-first game (like a PWA) sidesteps store fees entirely on web and only faces them for app-store-installed copies.

### Gaps
- No primary Stripe or Xsolla 2025–2026 report with payer-shift or conversion figures for web shops was retrievable (Xsolla release 403'd; Appcharge/Stash reports not found).
- No data on web-shop performance for small studios; all share-of-revenue figures are from top publishers.
- Final Apple commission rate and Judge Donato's ruling on Google's fees (post-July 2026 hearing) were not found; status after June 2026 unknown.
