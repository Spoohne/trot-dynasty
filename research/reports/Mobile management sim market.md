# Harness racing's empty niche rewards fairness, not scale

Trot Dynasty enters a mobile management-sim market that is huge on installs, thin on per-player revenue, and almost empty in its specific lane: harness racing has exactly one mobile competitor, Off and Pacing, with under 1,000 ratings per store and no feature update since a July 2025 billing fix. Simulation is a ~$4.8B/yr IAP genre growing ~11% and the second-largest mobile genre by downloads, but its median player churns fast (D1 ~17%, D28 ~0.5%) and spends little; a realistic plan for a small-studio manager is roughly $1-5 per monthly active user, with 1-3% of installs ever paying. The tycoon cohort skews female and 25-44 while racing and sports skew male, so presentation will decide which audience Trot Dynasty actually attracts. Paid acquisition is expensive for this genre (US iOS CPI plausibly $3-6, implying $10-20 per day-1 returner), so a sub-$5K/month budget should go to creators, Reddit clips and a free web build rather than ads; every indie postmortem with numbers shows a single creator hit outperforming all other channels. The pass-plus-cosmetics model has weak direct evidence at indie scale, but it is now structurally favored: Google's proposed 9% fee tier for cosmetics versus 20% for gameplay-advantage purchases, zero-commission US web link-outs on both stores, and a competitive set whose most-hated pattern is premium currency tied to horse upkeep. The gaps competitors leave open, a single nightly card with a day-ahead program, published race math, cosmetic-only spending, and a modern mobile-friendly web app, map almost one-for-one onto what Trot Dynasty already builds.

## Simulation leads installs at $4.8B, but players spend little and churn fast

The mobile market as a whole stalled in 2025: **50.4B downloads (-7.2%) against $81.75B of IAP (+1.3%)**, with growth captured almost entirely by the top 50 games and Strategy ($20.2B) the only genre growing on revenue, downloads and time spent at once ([Sensor Tower via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/seven-things-the-2025-state-of-gaming-report-actually-tells-us)). Inside that flat market, Simulation is a volume genre: **9.8B installs in 2024, 6.2% of all game downloads**, second only to platformers/runners ([Sensor Tower via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-mobile-market-in-2025)), and its IAP revenue rose from **$4.4B to $4.8B (+11.2%) in 2025**, with work sims (+107%) and animal sims (+106%) the fastest-growing subgenres ([AppMagic via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)). "Simulators, puzzles, strategy" were also the biggest contributors to time-spent growth in 2025 ([Sensor Tower via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)). The sports-manager corner is far smaller: the last public figure put US sports-manager revenue at **~$12M a year (2020-21), growing 60% YoY**, and Sensor Tower later noted the subgenre kept raising net revenue while downloads shrank, "a shift toward high-value monetization" ([Sensor Tower](https://sensortower.com/blog/us-mobile-sports-games-650-million-revenue); [Sensor Tower genre page](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-android-top-5-sports%20games-revenue-nl-6058d8b0241bc16eb8555111)). The ratio of installs to dollars is the key read: Simulation is top-2 in downloads yet never appears among top revenue genres, so per-install yield sits well below Strategy and Puzzle.

Per-player economics confirm that. Median Simulation retention in GameAnalytics' 2024 data is **D1 ~17%, D7 ~2.4%, D28 ~0.5%**, below Strategy (D1 ~20%, D28 ~0.7%) and Sports (D1 ~19%, D28 ~0.8%); top-quartile games across all genres reach D1 ~27%, D7 7-8%, D28 2.6-3% ([GameAnalytics 2025](https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). Retention fell again in 2025 across all three windows, with the top 10% of games at ~40% D1 and 11-12% D7 and only the top 1% above 13% D30 ([GameAnalytics 2026 via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Payer conversion runs around **0.8% of users making any IAP** ([Gitnux](https://gitnux.org/mobile-games-statistics/)), with a ~3% midcore benchmark inside 14 days ([Bruin](https://getbruin.com/use-cases/mobile-gaming/midcore-payer-conversion-rate-vs-genre-benchmark/)) and an older 3-7% monthly rule of thumb ([Gamesbrief](https://gamesbrief.com/?p=7558)). Appodeal's US Android data puts hypercasual and simulation together at **~3% 30-day retention and ARPU ~$0.79** ([Game Industry Library](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/mobile-casual-benchmarks-report-2025)). No vendor publishes an absolute Simulation ARPDAU; the mid-core ranges that exist ($0.08-0.40, with $0.30-0.50 at the top) are directional ([AdReact](https://adreact.com/blog/arpdau-calculator-benchmark-guide/); [Adapty](https://adapty.io/glossary/arpdau/)). Platform matters more than genre: the App Store takes **$52.5B of revenue on 15% of installs** while Google Play takes $30B on 81% ([Sensor Tower via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026)), and Strategy ARPPU on iOS runs 4-8x Android ([AppMagic via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)). For a US/English-first game, iOS is the money platform and Android the reach platform.

The engagement shape of top-quartile mobile games is also worth noting because it fits a scheduled race night: **5-6 sessions a day of ~5 minutes, ~23 minutes daily, with each weekend day carrying ~16% of weekly playtime** versus 13-14% per weekday ([GameAnalytics 2026 via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Desktop-browser players look different: PC medians are **18-minute sessions, 1.7 sessions a day, DAU/MAU 4-5%**, which is the right yardstick for Trot Dynasty's web players ([GameAnalytics 2026 via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)). Web gaming itself is growing off a small base: Poki reports 100M monthly players, roughly 40% on mobile, with revenue up ~50% a year since 2020 ([Mobidictum](https://mobidictum.com/?p=209499); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-11-07/video-games-hottest-new-platform-is-an-old-one-websites)), and simulator games were among the fastest-growing web segments in 2025 at ~25% traffic growth ([GameDeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/press-release/new-study-reveals-the-growth-engagement-discoverability-and-value-driving-web-gaming-to-outperform-gaming-giants-and-social-media)). But most browser play is still desktop (28-32% mobile across top portals) and web revenue is largely ad-based ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/web-gaming-strikes-back/)); no credible source measures PWA-versus-native retention or conversion for the same game.

## Two audiences meet at the stable door: female tycoon players and male racing fans

Trot Dynasty straddles two demographic cohorts that rarely share a game. GameRefinery's 2022 snapshot found **women are 61% of the audience for resource-optimization mechanics in tycoon and crafting games**, while men are 76-84% of 4X strategy and synchronous PvP players; both groups cluster at 25-44 and share the motivation to optimize resources, though tycoon players prefer the more casual titles ([GameRefinery via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gamerefinery-users-motivations-in); [GameIndustryLibrary](https://gameindustrylibrary.com/documents/motivation-and-demographics-snapshot-report-2022/read)). Sports, strategy and racing remain male-dominated in every 2024-2025 source ([Sensor Tower via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026); [Business of Apps](https://businessofapps.com/?p=97879)). The broader mobile audience is balanced (52% female in AppLovin's Feb 2026 survey; average US gamer age 36) but casual and hypercasual players are older, with 60% over 35 ([AppLovin](https://www.metaply.ai/applovin/a1-who-plays-mobile-games); [GameLuster on ESA](https://gameluster.com/?p=122574)). Mintel reports middle-aged mobile gamers are the biggest spenders ([Mintel](https://store.mintel.com/report/us-mobile-gaming-market-report)).

The "adult horse game" audience that the Sensor Tower horse-game charts describe is a useful proxy. In the US iOS top five for Q2 2025, Rival Stars Horse Racing led at a **~$52.9K weekly revenue peak with 34-44K weekly active users**, Star Stable Online followed at $56.8K peak with ~111K weekly actives, and the rest (Horse Riding Tales, Star Equestrian, My Little Pony World) peaked at $7-19K a week ([Sensor Tower Q2 2025 US iOS](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q2-ios-top-5-horse games-revenue-us-60a7063328293296043b4e31)). That is the practical ceiling for an unlicensed horse title on one store in one country: tens of thousands of dollars a week at the top, single-digit thousands for the mid-pack. Breeding, management and decoration pull the female-leaning cohort; racing, competition and a nightly card pull the male-leaning one. Which cohort Trot Dynasty actually lands depends on its screenshots and store art more than its mechanics, and the market data says there is no need to choose: the tycoon audience is larger and the sports audience spends more per head.

## Passes and cosmetics beat cash packs on fees and sentiment, if not on whale revenue

Evidence that cosmetic-only or pass-led monetization works at indie scale in this genre is thin, and the report says so plainly: no primary source publishes a pass attach rate or cosmetic revenue share for simulation or sports-manager games in 2024-2026. The directional picture still favors Trot Dynasty's design. Battle passes are near-universal at the top, **86% of top-100 grossers** in GameRefinery's count ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/battle-pass-utilization-doubled-past-year/)), and Google reported premium passes drive 1-40% of revenue in top games ([Google Play](https://goo.gle/3htI67y)). Price points cluster tightly: **$4.99 for a monthly mobile pass, $9.99 for a ~two-month cross-platform pass**, with Diablo Immortal, Overwatch 2, Valorant and Disney Speedstorm all inside that band ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/new-world/season-pass-price); [AFK Gaming](https://afkgaming.com/esports/guide/overwatch-2-season-5-battle-pass-price-duration-skins-items-more)). AppMagic's 2025 data shows the smallest bundle drives the most transactions (a $2.99 bundle generated 16% of purchases in Zenless Zone Zero) while Strategy average transactions run ~$7 on Google Play and >$15 on the App Store ([AppMagic via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-mobile-games-monetization)).

The cash-pack model is what every horse competitor uses, and it is what their reviews resent. Horse Racing Manager sells ten packs from $0.99 to $99.99 and requires premium horseshoes to feed horses and keep extras racing; reviewers call the recurring cost a rip-off and complain packs failed to deliver promised SS horses ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1275199710)). Rival Stars gates breeding behind boosters that are "hard to get" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/rival-stars-horse-racing/breeding-guide)); Equestrian the Game draws "pay to win" comments ([game-solver](https://game-solver.com/equestrian-the-game/)). The pay-to-win cautionary tale at scale is Diablo Immortal: >$24M in two weeks, a peak above $70M a month, then a **41% drop in revenue per paying user by 2024** and ~$6-7M a month by 2026 ([ShaneTheGamer](https://www.shanethegamer.com/research/diablo-immortal/); [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/esports/record-diablo-immortal-revenues-shows-grim-future-video-games-blizzard-thrives-pay-to-win-features)). The counter-example is Retro Bowl, free with no ads and a one-time ~$1.39 unlock, which reached 7M US downloads with zero UA spend and is praised specifically for the fairness of that model ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/uk-based-new-star-games-takes-us-by-storm-with-retro-bowl); [MobileSyrup](https://mobilesyrup.com/2020/09/27/retro-bowl-football-game-review/)). The honest trade-off is the one the glossary states: cosmetics-only "doesn't capture as much whale spend but preserves community" ([MWM](https://mwm.ai/glossary/pay-to-win)), and even Marvel Rivals, a cosmetic-only game grossing ~$135M in its first month, had to roll back a $25 pass accelerator after it touched progression ([FandomWire](https://fandomwire.com/marvel-rivals-has-quietly-rolled-back-its-most-controversial-feature/)).

The platform-fee environment has shifted decisively toward Trot Dynasty's model. Google's proposed settlement tiers charge **20% on purchases giving a gameplay advantage but 9% on cosmetics, extra levels and most subscriptions**, with bundles and dual-use currency defaulting to the higher tier; Sensor Tower found 37 of the top 50 US Play grossers would land in the 20% bracket ([Outlook Respawn](https://respawn.outlookindia.com/gaming/gaming-news/top-50-mobile-games-may-face-20-fee-under-new-play-store-rules); [SD Times](https://sdtimes.com/mobile/googles-settlement-with-epic-games-may-lead-to-changes-for-android-devs/)). Google's June 2026 fee change separately cut the service fee to 10% on the first $1M and on all subscriptions ([Stash](https://www.stash.gg/blog/googles-lower-fees-announcement-june-2026-whats-really-going-on-and-what-us-developers-should-do)). On iOS, the Ninth Circuit's December 2025 ruling left US external payment links at **zero Apple commission until a court sets a "reasonable" cost-based rate** ([Neon](https://www.neonpay.com/blog/the-latest-epic-v.-apple-appeal-ruling-december-2025-what-changed-for-game-developers)), and web-shop platforms charge ~5% all-in against 15-30% in-store, with 72% of top grossers now running a direct storefront and big publishers routing 20-40% of revenue through it ([Konvoy](https://podcast.app/newsletter-mobile-web-shops-the-great-platform-unbundling-e425042000); [BusinessWire on Xsolla](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20211215005807/en/5116073/Mobile-Game-Developers---Take-Full-Control-of-Your-Game.-Xsolla-Introduces-Web-Shop-for-Mobile-Games)). A web-first game sells the pass and Markers at ~5% cost on the browser and only pays store fees on app-store copies; the prudent model still books a 10-20% fee returning once the pending rates land. One structural note for the Markers design: a currency that can buy anything beyond cosmetics risks Google's 20% tier, so keeping Markers strictly cosmetic is a fee decision as well as a design one.

## Ads cost $10-20 per returning player; one creator video beats them all

Paid acquisition is a poor fit for a niche sim on a small budget. Adjust's 2026 data puts North America gaming CPI at **$1.68 (+31%) and North America CPM at $15.98, the most expensive region "by a wide margin"**, with Strategy among the priciest genres at $1.03 globally against $0.56 all-genre ([Adjust via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/adjust-gaming-app-insights-report)). Singular puts US Android gaming CPI at $3.25, the highest country ([Singular via GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/singular-trends-in-mobile-game-marketing)), and Liftoff reports simulation, action and racing games see roughly **10x higher CPIs on iOS than Android**, with casual D30 ROAS of 47% on iOS versus 15% on Android ([Liftoff](https://liftoff.ai/?p=35032)). Agency and vendor tables put US simulation CPI around $3.75-4.50 on iOS ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/cpi-ipm-roas-benchmarks-optimizing-ad-spend/)) and Meta installs at $2-4 ([RocketShip HQ](https://www.rocketshiphq.com/?p=5288)). Combining those with the 27% all-genre D1 retention Adjust reports, a $5,000 monthly budget buys on the order of 800-1,600 US iOS installs and 200-450 day-1 returners, an implied **$10-20 per returning player**; this is derived arithmetic, not a published benchmark, and no vendor publishes cost per paying user for the genre. Global gaming UA spend hit $25B in 2025 with nearly half flowing to the US, while top advertisers now produce 2,400-2,600 creative variants a quarter ([AppsFlyer](https://www.appsflyer.com/company/newsroom/pr/gaming-marketing/)); an indie cannot compete on that axis.

The organic record for sim indies is consistent even though every numbered case is a Steam funnel. Indoorlands, a theme-park management sim, launched 1.0 with 13K wishlists and sold only 1,250 copies in week one, then an unplanned Let's Game It Out video (5.4M views) sold **1,600 copies in a week, more than launch week**; a major sale "didn't shake the earth" and the project never recouped its ~€200K cost ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=22212)). Gambler's Table went 0 to 10K wishlists in 18 days with ~75% of views from the itch.io popular algorithm and ~10% from Reddit, and every later spike tracked a YouTuber with 30K+ subscribers; its Discord had ~70 users at launch, ~10 of whom played ([Greenpixels devlog](https://greenpixels.itch.io/gamblers-table/devlog/945763/wishlists-0-to-10000-in-just-three-weeks-)). Deep Pixel Melancholy reached 10K wishlists in 40 days with zero paid spend, Reddit converting best by UTM and a slow-opening teaser named the "main mistake" ([WN Hub](https://wnhub.io/news/stores-and-publishing/item-48313)). The baseline expectations are sober: a typical non-breakout Reddit post yields **100-150 wishlists**, a genre-subreddit clip 0-150, and the median Next Fest demo ~200 ([Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/reddit-indie-game-marketing); [Steam Page Analyzer wishlists](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/blog/how-to-get-steam-wishlists)). Paid TikTok "rarely pays off" for indies and dev clips typically stay under 1,000 views, while bug and glitch clips are among the best-performing content ([presskit.gg](https://presskit.gg/field-guides/tiktok-indie-game-marketing)). The hard part for Trot Dynasty is that a text-heavy management screen is the worst case for a five-second clip; the replay, a photo finish, or a longshot upset is the shareable asset, and the channel mix tilts toward 30-60 personally contacted mid-size creators plus harness-racing's own press and forums.

Web-first discovery works differently and has no measured uplift from a store listing. Browser games spread through shared URLs with one-click start, portals (Poki 100M monthly, CrazyGames 60M), SEO and communities, while a PWA is invisible to app-store search ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/nuspeko-vladeta-marinkovic-reveals-secret-to-making-viral-browser-games/); [abratabia](https://abratabia.com/mobile-web-games/distribution.php)). Poki's self-commissioned 2026 survey says **62% of web players later downloaded or bought a game they found on the web** and 53% of mobile studios plan to port to browser within a year ([Poki](https://poki.com/blog/state-of-web-gaming-report-2026)); the conversion runs web-to-store, which argues for the hybrid already chosen: a shareable URL per race result as top of funnel, a store-wrapped build later for the players who only find games in stores and for push notifications.

## Competitors leave five gaps, and harness racing is unoccupied

The competitive set is small and its shape is clear. The direct harness competitor, **Off and Pacing**, sells coin packs from $0.99 to $54.99, has 148 iOS ratings and ~785-936 across aggregators at 3.7-3.8/5 with "polarized" reception, and its last update was a billing fix in July 2025; reviews praise the claiming/buying/breeding mix and the Discord community while calling the loop "fun but repetitive," asking for feed choices and more training, and complaining about overnight races and alleged rigged draws ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/off-and-pacing-horse-racing/id1185495888); [Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/com-offandpacing-www)). The thoroughbred leader **Rival Stars Horse Racing** has passed 50M downloads across mobile and PC VR, is expanding to consoles in 2026, and earns ~$50-75K in US weekly revenue at peak, yet critics find it "rarely feels engaging where it counts" ([PikPok](https://pikpok.com/news/rival-stars-horse-racing-vr-edition-opens-pre-orders-on-january-20-2025/); [Sensor Tower Q1 2025](https://sensortower.com/blog/2025-q1-unified-top-5-horse%20games-units-us-60a7063328293296043b4e31); [Metacritic](https://legacy.prod.metacritic.com/game/switch/rival-stars-horse-racing/critic-reviews)). **Horse Racing Manager** (Third Time) is the management-first thoroughbred title with 6.4K iOS ratings at 4.6, live PvP every five minutes, and the upkeep-in-premium-currency complaints above ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id1275199710)). Its sibling **Photo Finish LIVE** proves a scheduled racing loop with stakes can drive heavy spend, **$30M+ in entry fees since 2023**, a monthly Virtual Kentucky Derby and a weekly Saturday pick'em, but its Solana dependency keeps it off mainstream stores ([Third Time Games](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=7396); [KentuckyDerby.com](https://www.kentuckyderby.com/horses/news/photo-finish-live-unlocks-a-new-generation-of-horse-racing-fans/)). The adjacent sports managers show the fee-free end of the spectrum: Top Eleven (300M+ registered, Take-Two) sells token packs plus a seasonal battle pass ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordeus); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/top-eleven-be-football-manager/id459035295)), Motorsport Manager Mobile 3 is a $1.99-7.99 paid app, and Retro Bowl is the praised one-time unlock. The web sims are either 1998-era (SIMHorseRacing.com, free, 16-week game year) or crypto-native.

| Gap | Evidence | Trot Dynasty position |
|---|---|---|
| Fairness and transparency | "Rigged" accusations in Off and Pacing and HRM reviews; only Photo Finish publishes its race math ([Third Time](https://thirdtimegames.com/?p=4328)) | Publish explainable race math; no hidden-outcome currency |
| One fixed nightly card | Competitors run 24/7 or every-5-minutes loops; players ask for no 1-7 AM races ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/off-and-pacing-horse-racing/id1185495888)) | 8 PM NY race night with day-ahead program is unoccupied |
| Breeding depth without gates | Praised everywhere, gated by scarce boosters (Rival Stars) or premium currency (HRM) | Breeding paid in game cash only |
| Cosmetic-only spending | Every horse F2P title sells currency, horses or upkeep; none sells cosmetics only | Pass + Markers; no cash for horses |
| Modern web-first play | Only SIMHorseRacing and Photo Finish are web-first; neither is mobile-friendly | PWA now, Capacitor store build later |

The pick'em layer has precedent and runs as a series at Photo Finish, and scheduled real-clock racing is accepted when the owner can watch it; the complaint is about races nobody is awake for. A single evening card with a program drawn the day before is the one loop no competitor offers.

## Conclusion

The market data reframes what success looks like for Trot Dynasty. The ceiling is not Rival Stars' 50M downloads; it is a deep, small, loyal audience, the "high-value monetization on shrinking installs" pattern Sensor Tower already sees in sports managers, where a few thousand weekly actives on one store can produce five-figure weekly revenue. Against that goal, median Simulation retention (D28 ~0.5%) is the wrong benchmark and the Sports/Strategy medians or the PC-browser pattern (18-minute sessions, DAU/MAU 4-5%) are the right ones; a game that hits D1 30-40% and D30 4%+ is top-10% of all mobile games, and a daily race night is the cleanest mechanism yet observed for getting there.

Two implications go beyond the competitive table. First, the fee environment has quietly made Trot Dynasty's monetization design the cheapest to run: cosmetic SKUs land in Google's 9% tier, subscriptions in its 10% tier, and web sales at ~5%, so a cosmetic-only Markers economy sold mostly on the web keeps roughly 20-25 more points per dollar than a cash-pack competitor on the same gross, which partly offsets the whale revenue it forgoes. Second, the acquisition record says the launch asset is not the store page but a single clip of a race, a photo finish or a longshot upset, placed in front of 30-60 mid-size creators and harness racing's own press, which covered Off and Pacing unprompted in 2017. The biggest unknown is whether the breeding-and-barn cohort or the race-night cohort shows up first; the data suggests building the art for the former and the schedule for the latter, and measuring which one pays for the pass.
